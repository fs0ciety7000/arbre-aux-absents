*A sheet folded in four, with its envelope: “Miss Danela, the Dalvarin house, Dalvica, via Brénica
(Thalberie)”, Velmarchois stamp, Hautcorvin postmark of 11 April 1921, a Thalbère arrival postmark.
Black ink, the same hand as the letter of 1912. Returned to Honoré in 1960.*

Hautcorvin, 10 April 1921

Danela,

My father died on 7 January. I did not write sooner because I did not know what I had the right to
write; and then I understood that there was no longer anyone I could ask for that right.

Come back.

I was offered the directorship and I refused it. I am staying at the furnace; it is the only place
where I do no harm to anyone. The firm will be run by a company secretary and by the board, and the
board is me. The mayor says the decree of 1916 fell with the peace; if it has not, I shall bring it
down myself.

The engraving workshop is yours. The wheels have stood still for five years; nobody here knows how
to finish a laurel leaf. The lodging above the workshop is empty; I have had the stove redone.

And the boy will go to school with Bastien. Bastien will be eleven in May, your son is eight: they
will fight, and they will learn to read together. I told Eugénie. She answered: “Let him come, I need
someone to hold my hatboxes.”

My mother asks that new books be bought for him, and that I not be told how much they cost.

I shall wait for you at the pass on the first Sunday in May, with the carriage.

::signature[H.]
