::stamp[OFFICE OF MAÎTRE JULIEN ESCANDE — NOTARY AT HAUTCORVIN — 3 PLACE DU MARCHÉ]

Hautcorvin, Monday 29 December 1975

To the Director of the Civil Re-registration Office, Aubrelieu.

**Subject:** estate of Mr Honoré Tessonnal, master glassmaker, died at Hautcorvin on 19 December
1975. Transmission of a certified copy of the will and notice of a sealed packet.

Dear Sir,

Mr Honoré Tessonnal’s will names your Office; I am therefore bound to send you a copy of it, which I
do herewith.

I must point out at once that I am the son-in-law of Mrs Colombe Brossal, the deceased’s daughter. I
have withdrawn from advising her in any way in this estate, and I send you here only what the law
obliges me to send.

**1. The will.** On 20 June 1973 Mr Honoré Tessonnal deposited at my office a holograph will,
written, dated and signed entirely in his own hand, on a single sheet, in an open envelope. After his
death, I drew up the record of its opening and description on 23 December. Here is the full text:

> ::hand[This is my will.]
>
> ::hand[I leave the whole of my estate to my living children, in equal shares.]
>
> ::hand[I entrust my papers to the Civil Re-registration Office.]
>
> ::hand[I have nothing else to say that can be written here.]
>
> ::hand[Done at Hautcorvin, the twentieth of June nineteen hundred and seventy-three.]
>
> ::hand[Honoré Tessonnal, master glassmaker]

Certified true copy enclosed. The original remains among my original deeds.

**2. The sealed packet.** The same day, Mr Honoré Tessonnal handed me a kraft-paper envelope, closed
with three seals of red wax bearing his monogram, on which he had written the following
superscription:

> ::hand[To whichever of my children did not bear my name. Not to be opened until after the decision of the Civil Re-registration Office.]

I do not know what it contains. I shall not open it before the time it sets, and I shall hand it
only to the person whom the Office’s decision designates. It is kept in the office safe.

**3. The papers.** The deceased leaves in his study at the Tessonnal house a number of personal
papers (correspondence, photographs, a diary that is not in his hand). In accordance with the will, I
am having an inventory drawn up and shall have them brought to you in the course of January.

**4. The heirs.** To my knowledge, and subject to whatever the Office may establish, the deceased
leaves a daughter, Mrs Colombe Brossal, née Tessonnal, and a son, Mr Bastien Tessonnal, struck off in
1938, recorded as dead in the Kessaar penal colony in 1944, a death that was never entered in the
civil register. I would remind you that Article 12 of the Act of 14 May 1973 provides for an absentee
who died in detention to be represented by those of his descendants whose parentage is established.
I know of no established descendant.

I remain at the Office’s disposal for any requisition.

Yours faithfully,

::signature[J. Escande]

Maître Julien Escande, notary at Hautcorvin

::hand[P.S. I am told at Vernezac that a petition has been pending before the Office since the autumn of last year, in the name of Mrs Agnès Neviot. I know no more than that, and it is not for me to know more. I mention it so that nothing is overlooked. J. E.]
