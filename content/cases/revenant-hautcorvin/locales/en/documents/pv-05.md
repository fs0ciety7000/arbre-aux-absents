::stamp[COMMUNE OF VELLAAK — TOWN HALL — EXECUTION OF LETTERS ROGATORY]

**RECORD OF HEARING OF A WITNESS**

In the year nineteen hundred and seventy-six, on Wednesday the eighteenth of February, at ten
o’clock in the morning, at the town hall of Vellaak, in the council chamber,

before us, town clerk, acting by delegation of the mayor, in execution of the letters rogatory of
the Civil Re-registration Office dated 13 February (file 76-0012), whose written questions we have
received and read to the witness in their order,

there appeared **Mr Teodor Haask**, born on 3 April 1912, residing at Vellaak, retired, formerly a
warder at the Kessaar penal colony, who states that he is neither related to nor connected by
marriage with the claimant or the objecting party, and that he has no interest in the case.

The witness swears to tell the truth.

---

**Question 1.** — Did you know the prisoner entered at Kessaar under No. 0214, Bastien Tessonnal?

**A.** — Yes. 0214 was the glassblower. We called him that because he talked about his furnace all
the time. The right hand burnt across the back, an old burn, quite smooth. Eyes as pale as water.
A broken front tooth.

**Question 2.** — Did you know the prisoner entered under No. 1171, Andrin Dalvarin?

**A.** — Yes. 1171 was the Thalbère. He arrived in the autumn of 1941. They put him in with 0214,
in cell 14. He had dark eyes and eyebrows like a bar. He engraved things on whatever he could find,
with a nail – on the mess tins, on the wood of the bunk. They’d take the nail off him; he’d find
another. In the summer of 1943 he burnt his left hand at the lime kiln. The infirmary treated him.
The scar stayed.

**Question 3.** — What do you know of the death of 0214, on 9 March 1944?

**A.** — I was on duty at the infirmary that night. 0214 had had a bad chest since February. He
died at about eleven at night. Dr Hervel was there; he certified the death and wrote the number
himself, in his book, reading it off the tag. It was me who took the tag off, in front of him. It
said 0214.

**Question 4.** — Was 1171 present?

**A.** — Yes. They’d let him come, he carried the water. He was sitting on the floor against the
bed. He was crying. He didn’t let go of 0214’s hand until the doctor told him to.

**Question 5.** — The claimant states that on that night he exchanged his number tag with the dead
man’s, in the infirmary, “at night, when no one was looking”. What do you say to that?

**A.** — There was the lamp, the doctor and me. We were looking. Nobody swapped anything between
those two. The doctor was there, and the Thalbère was crying.

**Question 6.** — What became of 1171?

**A.** — He stayed till the end. He was released on 11 April 1952, with his own tag, under his own
number. I saw him leave for Port-Sénane. He still had the mark on his left hand, and he didn’t say
anything any more.

**Question 7.** — You are shown the photograph attached to the petition of 12 January 1976. Do you
recognise this man?

**A.** — I won’t swear to a photograph taken thirty years later. The island changes a man more than
the years do. Those two, at the end, they were just as thin as each other, held their mess tins the
same way, and from behind, in the yard, you’d mistake one for the other. Him, in your photograph,
he looks at me like someone I knew; I couldn’t tell you which. I know one thing only: 0214 – it was
me who closed his eyes.

**Question 8.** — Do you have anything to add?

**A.** — No. I’m not asking anyone for anything.

---

Having heard this read over, the witness confirms it and signs with us. Closed at 10.40.
