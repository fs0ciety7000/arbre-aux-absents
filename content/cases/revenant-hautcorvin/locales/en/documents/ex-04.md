**Expert report** — requisition from the Civil Re-registration Office, file 76-0012

Aubrelieu, Tuesday 24 February 1976

*Question put: were the glasses below engraved by the same hand?*

## Documents examined

| No. | Item | Date | Provenance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Regent's Cup**: footed crystal cup, profile of the Regent within a laurel wreath; workshop signature “Hautcorvin Glassworks, 1941” | Sept. 1941 | National Archives, Regency exhibits, seal 41-1987-1 |
| 2 | Engraved glass tumbler, “To the glassblower's daughter — 14.II.1959”: the Pas, a she-bear and her cub | Feb. 1959 | lent by the joined claimant (file 74-0962) |
| 3 | Tumbler engraved “Laurier”, No. 24 in the catalogue of the Brénica Glassworks, engraved to the catalogue design by A. Dalvarin | 1974 | sent by the Brénica Glassworks at the Office's request |
| 4 | Cup engraved “Laurier”, No. 112 in the catalogue of the Hautcorvin Glassworks, engraved by Danela Dalvarin | 1925 | Museum collections, gift of 1962 |

Examined under a binocular magnifier (magnifications of 10 and 25), in raking light, with silicone
casts of the finest cuts.

## Observations

1. **The tool.** All four pieces are engraved with the copper wheel, with emery mixed in oil; the
   veins are gone over again with the diamond point. This is the technique of the old Vernezac
   workshop, which was hardly practised anywhere else in Velmarche after 1930.
2. **The leaf.** On pieces 1, 2 and 3, each laurel leaf (or blade of grass, on piece 2) is finished
   with a single flick of the point, **drawn from right to left**, which leaves at the tip a small
   tail curling to the left. On piece 4 the same flick of the point is there, shorter and heavier.
   The gesture is the same; the hand is not.
3. **The drawing.** On piece 2, the path over the Pas is shown with precision: three hairpin bends, a
   cross at the third, the gap. The engraver knew the crossing, or had learnt it by heart.
4. **The laurel on the Cup.** The wreath has forty-two leaves. On the left side, between the seventh
   and eighth leaves, three signs are hidden in the veins, six tenths of a millimetre high, invisible
   to the naked eye, legible under a ten-power lens: **B · T · 38**. The crossbar of the T closes in a
   loop to the left. The 1941 seal card records them as “suspicious marks”, without transcribing them.

## Conclusion

The same hand in 1941, 1959 and 1974: the way of finishing the laurel leaves with one flick of the
point, to the left. That hand learnt its craft from Danela Dalvarin.

Pieces 1 and 2 are not signed. Piece 3 is, by its catalogue.
