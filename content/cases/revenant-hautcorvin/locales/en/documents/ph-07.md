## Front

Print, 24 × 30 cm, grey, sharp in the centre, blurred at the edges; the bottom right-hand corner has
been torn off. Behind the group, the brick wall of the furnace hall and the mouth of Furnace No. 2,
its cast-iron door raised, letting out a glow that has burnt a white circle into the image. Sixteen
people in two rows: the first seated on office chairs lined up in the yard, the second standing.

**Front row**, from left to right. A man of about fifty with a wrestler’s build, hair swept back and
thinning, bushy eyebrows, a dimple in his left cheek; his huge hands, marked with burns, rest flat
on his knees. A woman of about thirty in a dark costume, with very pale eyes, a dimple in her left
cheek, a glass brooch engraved with a [[dove|colombe]] on her lapel. A man with a pencil moustache, hair slicked
down with a parting, stiff collar, white pocket handkerchief, his right eyebrow raised. A woman of
about fifty in a leather apron, a greying plait coiled into a crown, straight, thick eyebrows almost
joined, an embroidered scarf. A man of about fifty with a thin moustache, stiff collar, hair
plastered down, grey at the temples. Then three workmen in smocks.

**Second row**, standing, from left to right. Four glassblowers in vests, pipes on their shoulders.
An old workman of about sixty, bareheaded, thin grey hair, grey moustache, an unlit lantern in his
hand. Then two young men of about twenty-five, side by side, of the same build.

The first, broad in the cheek, has pushed his canvas cap back on his head; a lock of hair stands up
in a cowlick in the middle of his forehead; his very pale eyes look almost white on the print, and
he is laughing, his lips parted over a chipped front tooth. A dimple in his left cheek. His right
arm is round his neighbour’s shoulders, and the back of his hand, flat on that shoulder, gleams with
a broad scar.

The second has the same oval face and the same dimple in the left cheek; but his eyes are dark,
under straight, thick eyebrows that almost meet, and the line of his black hair is even, with no
cowlick. He wears the engravers’ leather apron, tied at the neck, and a loupe hanging from a cord.
His hands, folded over the apron, are unmarked.

At the edge of the image, cut off by the tear, one last workman of whom only a shoulder can be seen.

Beneath the image, on the card, a typed caption; the torn corner has carried off the end of the
second row with it:

Coste, Imbert. Second row: Messrs Barrial, Monteil, Sagnes, Rouvel, T. Mallet, B. Tessonnal,
