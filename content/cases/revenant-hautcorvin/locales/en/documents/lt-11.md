*Two sheets of ruled writing paper, a round, careful hand, blue ink, in the hand of the woman writing
it down; at the foot of the second, an initial in pencil, very faint, in another hand. Mentioned by
Mrs Agnès Neviot in her petition of 1974, produced at her hearing of 11 February 1976; certified copy
in file 74-0962, the original returned to her.*

Vernezac, 6 January 1969

My girl, my little Agnès,

I am telling you this through someone else’s hand, because mine won’t any more. She writes what I
say, nothing else; you can trust her.

Your father’s name was Bastien Tessonnal. He was a glassblower at the hall, the master’s son. You
have his cowlick. You have his eyes too; I never told you, because I would have had to tell the rest.

They accused him of the fire of 1937. He went up to the refuge and I hid him all winter. They took
him at the Pas, in May, one night, and I got away. You were already there; I didn’t know it yet.

A man came in 1953 to tell me he had died out there. I never quite believed it. I believed it with
my head, not with the rest of me. That man also told me, as a message from him, that your father
didn’t set the fire. I am telling you as it was told to me.

Victorin had no part in it. He brought you up as best he could, and he did more than you know. Don’t
ask him anything; he won’t say anything.

If the Thalbère comes back, let him in.

Your mother
