Hautcorvin, Thursday 22 January 1976

To the Chief Clerk of the Civil Re-registration Office, for the Re-registration Commission.

**Subject:** petition No. 76-0012 for the re-registration of a living absentee, lodged on 12 January
1976 by a man calling himself Bastien Tessonnal. **Notice of objection** (Act of 14 May 1973, Art. 6).

Dear Sir,

I have the honour to lodge an objection to the petition referred to above, in the name and on behalf
of Mrs Colombe Brossal, born Colombe Tessonnal on 23 July 1906 at Hautcorvin, director of the
Hautcorvin Glassworks, widow of Mr Aimé Brossal, residing there at the Tessonnal house, rue Haute;
and also in the name of her children, Mr Hugues Brossal, engineer at the Glassworks, and Mrs Isaure
Escande, who have joined her.

My client was notified of the petition on 16 January, together with a copy of the photograph attached
to it. She has instructed me to convey to you, before any other consideration, the following
statement, which she insisted on writing herself and which I reproduce without altering a word:

> “This man is not my brother. My brother had our mother’s eyes.”

**1. As to identity.** My client saw her brother every day of her life until 27 November 1937. She
does not recognise the claimant. She notes that the description taken by your registry gives him
brown eyes; those of Bastien Tessonnal were a very pale grey, like his mother’s, and nobody in
Hautcorvin was ever unaware of it. The claimant produces no document in the name he claims. He
produces, on the contrary, a re-registration card in another name.

**2. As to the death.** The Kessaar committal registers, which have been released to the Office,
record the death of the prisoner Tessonnal on 9 March 1944. My client does not accept that an error
by the Regency should be presumed whenever it happens to suit a stranger.

**3. As to the will.** Mr Honoré Tessonnal drew up the will of 20 June 1973 in his ninety-third year.
My client reserves all rights as to its scope, and in particular as to the scope of a packet that no
one has opened and whose address is already the talk of the town. She asks me to repeat what she
tells anyone who questions her: “My father had only two children.”

**4. As to the memory of the late Mr Aimé Brossal.** Remarks attributed to the claimant, which part
of the local press is, we are told, about to reproduce, link the name of the late Mr Aimé Brossal,
company secretary of the firm for thirty-two years, with the 1937 blaze. My client and her children
reserve the right to prosecute their author, under whatever name the Commission may give him.

On these grounds, Mrs Colombe Brossal asks the Commission to **reject petition No. 76-0012**, to rule
that the death certificate entered in the Kessaar register is authoritative until the contrary is
proved, and to reserve all her rights as to the execution of the will.

My client remains at the Office’s disposal to be heard, at Hautcorvin, as her health does not permit
her to travel in winter. She does not wish to be confronted with the claimant.

Yours faithfully,

::signature[G. Vallin]

Gérard Vallin, barrister
