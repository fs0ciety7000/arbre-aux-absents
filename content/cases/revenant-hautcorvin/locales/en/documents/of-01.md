::stamp[REPUBLIC OF VELMARCHE — CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILIATIONS DEPARTMENT]

Aubrelieu, Monday 2 February 1976

**Subject:** file No. 76-0012 — petition for the re-registration of a living absentee, joined with
file No. 74-0962 (petition for the restoration of parentage).

**To:** permanent rapporteur, office 12, third floor.

**Enclosures:** items 2 to 7, joined file 74-0962 (see schedule at the end of this note).

Dear Rapporteur,

You have not been on probation for a year now. I am therefore entrusting you with a file I would
not entrust to a probationer: there is a living man in it, who sits every morning on the bench in
the corridor, waiting to be told who he is.

## 1. The petition

On Monday 12 January, a man presented himself at the registry and lodged a petition for the
re-registration of a living absentee (Act of 14 May 1973, Art. 4). He declares himself to be
**Bastien Tessonnal**, born on 14 May 1910 at Hautcorvin, son of the firm’s master glassmaker,
struck off by Removal List No. 9 of 26 March 1938, committed to Kessaar. The committal registers
record this prisoner as having died in the penal colony on 9 March 1944. The claimant maintains that
this death is an error, that he has lived ever since under another man’s name, and that he now asks
for his own (item 2). He was heard for the first time on 13 January (item 6).

The man has an answer for everything. The clerk, who has seen a few in his time, told me he answered
too well. That is not a document; it is a reason to read the documents.

## 2. The objection

Mrs **Colombe Brossal**, director of the Hautcorvin Glassworks, sister of Bastien Tessonnal, formed
an objection on 22 January through Maître Gérard Vallin, barrister of the Aubrelieu Bar (item 3). She
disputes the claimant’s identity and, in advance, whatever interpretation may be put on her father’s
will.

## 3. The will

Mr **Honoré Tessonnal**, master glassmaker, died at Hautcorvin on 19 December last, in his
ninety-sixth year. By a holograph will of 20 June 1973, he leaves the whole of his estate “to my
living children, in equal shares” and entrusts his papers to the Office. His notary further reports
a sealed packet, addressed “to whichever of my children did not bear my name”, which is not to be
opened until after our decision (items 4 and 5).

I need not tell you what that phrase has already set Hautcorvin saying. I ask you only to remember
that the Office does not read sealed packets: it reads registers.

## 4. The joined petition

Mrs **Agnès Neviot**, nurse, residing at Vernezac, lodged on 3 October 1974 a petition for the
restoration of paternal parentage, which has remained in abeyance for want of documents (joined file
74-0962). She says she is the daughter of Bastien Tessonnal. The claimant in 76-0012 declares her to
be his daughter. The two files will be decided together: I ordered them joined this morning.

## 5. What the Commission expects of you

You will draw up the table of the **descendants of Évariste Tessonnal**, the testator’s father, and
of the **maternal ancestry of the joined claimant**. The regulation form (form ORC-3) requires the
three ordinary headings for each person: identity (usual given name and birth surname; married names
appearing in the certificates are admitted), portrait identification, occupation. By occupation, the
Office means the trade learned and practised, as the civil register records it: not the rank held in
a workshop, nor the job done for a season. A foreman remains a turner; a sergeant remains a mason.

Two slots in the table are not ordinary.

- **Bastien Tessonnal’s slot is filled in from the claimant’s statement; nothing in it is
  established.** Article 4 allows the claimant to fill in his own card, with his photograph and his
  occupation. A filled slot is not an established slot. You will remove from it whatever does not
  hold.
- **One slot bears a question mark.** The testator’s packet presupposes a child who did not bear his
  name. I have had it entered in the table, under Mr Honoré Tessonnal, with neither name nor face. I
  draw no conviction from it; I have merely left a space free.

Article 6 of the Act is unchanged: *no identity is admitted unless two concordant documents
establish it.* The Commission stamps coherent sets; it does not stamp hunches.

## 6. Your means

- The **LX-2 Reader**, which you know. The collections of the upper Orvelle valley have just been
  transferred to it: municipal registers of Hautcorvin and Vernezac, including the death indexes, the
  Glassworks collection, customs. Read the computing department’s note (item 7) before you type
  anything at all.
- The Regency’s **restricted collections** (Census and Removals Bureau, Kessaar committal registers)
  are released only on an exact given name and surname (Art. 9). The Reader will not help you find
  them; nor will it stop you asking for them.
- The investigating clerk, Mr Fernand Ollier, conducts the hearings. He will hear the claimant again
  on Thursday 5 February, on the written questions of the objecting party. Yours must reach him by
  the 3rd at the latest.
- The press, lastly, since it got there before us. *The Corvins Sentinel* of Saturday 24 January ran
  the headline “The Revenant of Hautcorvin”; the whole district read it before you did. Read it too.
  An article is not a document, but it tells you what the witnesses have already heard.

## 7. Deadline

The Commission’s sitting is set for **Monday 15 March 1976**. For the first time, your report will
not end with a table alone: the Commission will also ask you what is to be done with the petition.
You will have time to think about it. Not much.

I remain at your disposal, door 4, mornings.

Rosine Ferrandot, head of the Filiations Department
