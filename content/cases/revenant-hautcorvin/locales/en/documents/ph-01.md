## Front

Albumen print, 13 × 18 cm, mounted on grey card with one corner clipped. In the centre, under a
glass case with brass uprights, a great stepped lighthouse lens, taller than two men: its glass
rings catch the light from the glazed roof and leave white circles on the print that eat into the
image. Fringed drapes on either side; on a pedestal table at the foot of the plinth, an open casket
whose gilt fillets gleam.

To the left of the case, a man of about forty stands very straight: a short-trimmed chin-curtain
beard, hair parted in the middle, small round spectacles pushed up on his forehead, as if he had
just looked up from a calculation. A dimple hollows his left cheek. A waistcoat with a watch chain,
a medal pinned to his lapel.

To the right, a woman of about thirty with a fine-boned face and very fine, arched eyebrows, her
hair in a high chignon, a high collar fastened with a round brooch of gilded glass – a lens in
miniature. She holds by the hand a boy of seven or eight in a sailor suit, who is looking at the
lens rather than at the photographer; he too has a dimple in his left cheek, and in the middle of
his forehead a cowlick that lifts a lock of hair in spite of the wetted parting.

Printed in gold letters on the card: *Grand Exhibition of Aubrelieu, 1889 — Fire Arts Gallery —
Hautcorvin Glassworks: first-order lens for the Tennok light.*
