*Military correspondence letter-card, post-free, violet cachet “Passes Sector — 2nd Engineer
Company — seen”. Pencil, the fine, regular hand of a lens maker, which never strays over the
lines.*

**Sender:** Léonce Tessonnal, sapper, 2nd Engineer Company, Passes Sector.

**Addressee:** Mr Honoré Tessonnal, master glassmaker, rue Haute, Hautcorvin.

---

At the blockhouse, 24 September 1916

Old man,

Not much gets put right here, except the range. The captain found out I was a lens maker and has me
cleaning the field glasses for the whole company: it is the first time since Tennok that anyone has
asked me to look through glass for any reason other than taking aim.

On a clear day, from the ridge, you can see the smoke rising along the Dalva. I tell myself one of
those plumes is the old man’s charcoal stack, and that the little one is beside it, blowing on it to
look important. We fire in that direction. I aim wide. Don’t repeat it.

If I don’t come back, let them go on saying he is mine. Dead, it will cost me nothing, and it will
spare you all from having to find something else to say. Don’t pull that face: I know you, you pull
faces even in writing.

When you can, give the little Thalbère a kiss from his godfather. Tell him his godfather climbed up
into the lantern of the lighthouses and was not afraid.

Give Eugénie and the children a kiss. Keep the furnaces hot.

Your brother,

::signature[L. T., lens maker, sapper in the Passes]
