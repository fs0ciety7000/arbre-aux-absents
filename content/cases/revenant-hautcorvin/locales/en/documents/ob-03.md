*Tin lozenge box, “Corvins Lozenges, made with pine sap”, 14 × 9 × 4 cm, hinged lid, flaking green
paint, rust at the corners. A length of kitchen string knotted twice around it.*

## Registry inventory

::stamp[CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — REGISTRY — FILE 76-0012]

Drawn up on Monday 23 February 1976 at Vernezac town hall, in the presence of Mr Victorin Sarrade,
who hands over the box, and of F. Ollier, investigating clerk.

Mr Sarrade states: “It’s Lucie’s box. She shut it herself, in January ’69, when she knew. I haven’t
opened it.” The string is cut in front of him. The box contains, in the order in which the objects
come out:

1. A photograph, 6 × 9 cm: a young woman in front of a stone refuge, a whistle round her neck. On the back: “For B. — L. — Aug. 37”.
2. An envelope addressed to the Kessaar penal colony, struck through with an “Addressee absent” stamp, unopened.
3. A pencilled note, on the back of a Kessaar penal colony canteen voucher, folded in four.
4. A small notebook bound in black oilcloth. Mr Sarrade: “The sheep notebook.”
5. A tear of blown glass, three centimetres long, the tail intact; on the body, a B clumsily scratched with the point.
6. A horn whistle, split, broken at the mouthpiece, carved with a knife “J. N. — No. 7”. Mr Sarrade: “Her father’s. They found it on him, at the Pas, in February ’44.”

No other object. Items 1 to 4 are placed on file; the box, the tear and the whistle are placed under
seal (76-0012-S2) and will be returned to the family.

::signature[V. Sarrade — F. Ollier]
