**THE CORVINS SENTINEL** — Weekly of the upper valley — Hautcorvin — Saturday 15 January 1966
— 0.30 Fn

## THE VERNEZAC GUIDES BELOW THE PASS

### The Neviot rope found the wreck of the mail plane on Tuesday; the post carried down on men's backs

It had been known since Sunday evening that a mail plane was missing over the upper valley, caught in
the snowstorm. On Tuesday it was known where it lay. It was a rope from the Vernezac Guides' Company
that found it, late in the morning, at two thousand two hundred metres, on the north face, below the
Col de Corvenne: **Rémi Neviot**, guide No. 19, fifty-seven, and his son **Gilles Neviot**, guide
No. 31, who had set out from Vernezac at four in the morning through fresh snow.

What they found up there belongs to the commission of inquiry, and to the mourning of a family that
is not from here; we shall write nothing of it. We shall say only what the guides did next. The four
mailbags were intact. Rémi and Gilles Neviot carried them down on their backs to Vernezac, and the
guides insisted on delivering the valley's letters themselves on Wednesday, house by house. “It
wasn't for us to lose them,” said Rémi Neviot.

Meanwhile, at the village post office, **Miss Brigitte Neviot**, postmistress, daughter and sister of
the two guides, spent the night from Tuesday to Wednesday at the telegraph, passing on the messages of
the Company, the constabulary and the commission. She left her post only when the counter opened –
to serve at it.
