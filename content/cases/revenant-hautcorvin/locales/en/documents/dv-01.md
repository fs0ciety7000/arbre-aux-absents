*Three black-covered exercise books, out of the fourteen that make up the diary (Nos 3, 9 and 14).
Tight, sloping convent hand, violet ink, then blue from 1940 onwards. A holy picture serves as a
bookmark in notebook 3. On the flyleaf of each notebook, the same words:*

“Marguerite Tessonnal, in religion Sister Agathe, Hospitaller sister of Hautcorvin. For no one.”

## Notebook 3 (1911–1913)

**8 September 1912, evening.** I held my brother's son at the font. Not Léonce's: Honoré's. Léonce
has not been here all winter; he was at the far end of the country, in his lighthouse, turning
Papa's lens. May God forgive Papa, who wanted it so, and Léonce, who said yes and laughed, and me,
who signed in the register what I know not to be true.

The child is called Andrin. He did not cry under the water; he looked at the candle. He has the
dimple, our dimple, on the left, and not a single hair. Father Pradeil saw nothing, or he is
charitable. The mother stood at the back of the church, her leather apron over her Sunday dress; she
looked at Honoré once only, and Honoré looked at his shoes.

**Christmas 1912.** Mama went herself to take a layette to Vernezac. She says a child does not choose
his parish, and that she will pay for his schoolbooks when he has any. Papa does not know, or
pretends not to. In this house it comes to the same thing.

## Notebook 9 (1940–1944)

**26 September 1941.** The little Thalbère has been taken. They came for him at Vernezac on Wednesday
evening. People say it is because of the cup he engraved for the visit. Honoré went to see the
commissioner; he was not received. He came home and went to the furnace, at night, all alone.
Colombe says nothing. Aimé talks a great deal about the honour of the firm. I pray for him too: that
is my trade.

## Notebook 14 (1952–1964)

**16 April 1952.** H. has had the telegram. He says nothing. He blows glass. He has been in the hall
since dawn; you can hear the blowpipe from the road.

Danela had it brought to him from Dalvica. It is the little Thalbère who sent it, from Port-Sénane:
he is free. Bastien died out there on 9 March 1944. For eight years we prayed for a living man.

**July 1953.** H. has had a letter from Dalvica, in Thalbère. He read it in the chapel, translated
half of it for me and kept the other half. The boy has taken back his name at Vernezac. He still does
not know. Honoré will not tell him. I know him: he will make him a cup.
