*Letter from Lucie Neviot (married name Sarrade) to her brother: a sheet of squared paper torn from
an exercise book, folded in four, no envelope. On the back, in the same hand: “by Gilles, with the
sacks”. Papers of Rémi Neviot, given by his widow to the Vernezac Municipal Archives in 1972.*

::hand[At the refuge, Sunday evening]

::hand[Rémi,]

::hand[The Thalbère came. Thursday, about four o’clock. I thought it was him, in the doorway, with the sun behind him. The same way of standing crooked in a door, his shoulder against the jamb. I dropped the basin. Then he spoke.]

::hand[His hair is grey and his cheeks have gone. He said he had come out of that place, that he stayed with him to the end, in March 44. He gave me a paper from B. He knew things nobody knows, not you, not Papa when he was alive. He didn’t sit down. He drank a glass of water standing up. He went back over the pass, on foot, before nightfall.]

::hand[Victorin was in the woodshed. He saw him. He said nothing, he asked nothing, he carried the wood back up. I don’t know what I owe him, and I think I never will.]

::hand[Don’t say anything to Odette. She would only say again what she has been saying for fifteen years, and the little one would hear it. Let her say it if she likes, but not in front of the little one.]

::hand[I’m coming down on Saturday. Don’t come and fetch me, I don’t want anyone to see my eyes.]

::hand[Your sister, L.]
