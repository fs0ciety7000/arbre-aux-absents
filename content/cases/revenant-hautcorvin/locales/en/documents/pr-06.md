**THE BRENZAC MESSENGER** — Velmarchois edition — Brénica, Saturday 20 June 1953 — 0.20 Fn

## “THE KEEPER OF THE OURSE”

### Families of Brénica and the Dalva valley thank a Velmarchois woman

A letter reached our editorial office this week, delivered by hand. It has no author, or rather it
has thirty-eight: thirty-eight families from Brénica, from Dalvica and from the villages along the
Dalva, Thalbère and Velmarchois, who signed one after another at the foot of the sheet, some of them
with a cross. They ask us to publish it in full. We do so.

> “During the years when Velmarche struck a part of its children off its registers, men, women and
> children came to us on foot, at night, by the Pas de l'Ourse. Over there they no longer had a name.
> Here, we let them keep one. Many have gone back since April of last year; others have stayed; all
> of them are alive.
>
> “We wish to thank Mrs Lucie Neviot, keeper of the Refuge de l'Ourse, and the memory of her father,
> the guide Jules Neviot, who died at the Pas one night in February 1944. They know what they did. We
> want others to know it.
>
> “People say there were a hundred and forty-two of them. She has never said so.”

We wished to meet the keeper of the Ourse. Mrs Neviot – who has borne the name of Mrs Sarrade since
her marriage, but whom the people here know by her maiden name – sent us her answer through a
Vernezac boy, who came all the way to Brénica over the pass with these words: “I keep a refuge.
People go in, they go out. I have nothing else to say.”

The Refuge de l'Ourse, built in 1911 by the Vernezac Guides' Company below the Pas, is kept every
summer. It is five hours' climb from Vernezac; and five hours down, by the Pas, to Dalvica.
