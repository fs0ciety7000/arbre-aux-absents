**RECORD OF HEARING No. 3 (CONFRONTATION WITH THE DOCUMENTS)**

In the year nineteen hundred and seventy-six, on Tuesday the second of March, at two o’clock in the
afternoon, in hearing room No. 3 of the Civil Re-registration Office, at Aubrelieu,

before us, Fernand Ollier, investigating clerk, on the instructions of the rapporteur in charge of
the file (office 12), the rapporteur being present,

there appeared **the claimant** in petition No. 76-0012, who has until now declared himself to be
Bastien Tessonnal; Maître Vallin, duly notified, has sent his apologies.

The claimant is acquainted, document by document, with the medical report of 10 February, the
handwriting examination of 16 February, the anthropometric report of 20 February and the statement
of the witness heard at Vellaak on 18 February; he is then shown Lucie’s box, handed in by Mr
Victorin Sarrade on 23 February, and the pencilled note on a canteen voucher that it contained.

---

*The claimant takes the note. He does not read it: he holds it. He remains silent for about four
minutes, by the clock. He asks for a glass of water, which is given to him.*

**A.** — He had his mother’s eyes, and I had mine. I didn’t lie about what he lived through. I lied
about who is telling it.

**Q.** — State your identity.

**A.** — Andrin Dalvarin. Born on 2 September 1912 at Vernezac. Son of Danela Dalvarin, engraver.
Glass engraver. Struck off in 1941, Kessaar’s number 1171. Re-registered at Vernezac on 3 June
1953, under my own name. I’ve never had another, except for these seven weeks.

**Q.** — And your father?

**A.** — They said it was my uncle Léonce Tessonnal, the lens maker, my godfather. I always called
him my uncle, because that’s what I’d been taught to say. He died when I was four. All I remember
is his spectacles.

**Q.** — Who died in the infirmary at Kessaar on 9 March 1944?

**A.** — Bastien Tessonnal. My brother. People said cousin; people said the two brothers of the
Combe; I say my brother. The warder told you the truth. I was holding his hand. The tags – I never
touched them. I made that up in December, in my kitchen, at Dalvica.

**Q.** — Why?

**A.** — Your Office wrote in November to “A. Dalvarin, Dalvica” about the girl’s petition. To ask
him what? To say her father was dead? A Thalbère, an old lag from the colony, against an empty line
in a register: you’d have filed it away, and you’d have been right, it’s your law. A living father
who comes to the counter to acknowledge his daughter – that, you couldn’t file away. And then the
old man died in December. There was no one left in Hautcorvin to look me in the eye, except
Colombe. I knew Colombe would say no. I didn’t know she’d say it so fast.

**Q.** — You knew everything about him.

**A.** — He gave it to me. Two and a half years, at night, in cell 14. The cat on the rue Haute,
the glass tear, the path, the cross at the third bend, the slab that rings. He made me recite it.
He said: “If you get out and I don’t, somebody has to know.” I got out. I knew.

**Q.** — Why did you declare your occupation as night foreman?

**A.** — It’s the last time I saw him work. At the furnace, the night of the blaze. I didn’t know
the list had put glassblower. It’s the only thing about him I didn’t know.

**Q.** — Do you know who set fire to the hall?

**A.** — He knew. The night watchman knew. I wasn’t there: I won’t say what I didn’t see. You have
the registers.

**Q.** — And the Regent’s Cup?

**A.** — It’s in one of your cartons. Look at the laurel, with a ten-times loupe. I have nothing to
add on that.

**Q.** — What are you asking for, now?

**A.** — That the girl doesn’t pay for me. Agnès Neviot never asked for anything but a name. I
brought her a false one, because I had no true one to give her. If you have a true one, give it to
her. Mine – do what you like with it.

*The claimant signs the present record with his own name, without hesitating.*

---

Closed at 15.50.

F. Ollier, investigating clerk

*Note.* At 16.10, at the claimant’s request and with the rapporteur’s agreement, Mrs Agnès Neviot,
who was waiting in the corridor, is shown into hearing room No. 3. The clerk withdraws. No record
is drawn up of this meeting, which lasted an hour and a quarter.
