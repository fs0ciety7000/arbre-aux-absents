**THE HERALD** — Organ of the Velmarchois Order — Tuesday 9 September 1941 — 40 sous

## THE REGENT AT HAUTCORVIN

### The upper valley acclaims the Regent; the Glassworks present him with a crystal cup

*“The frontier is not a line on a map. It is a people standing upright behind it.” — The Regent, at
Hautcorvin, on 7 September.*

Hautcorvin had risen before daybreak. On Sunday, from seven o'clock, the high street was hung with
grey and white, and the mountain folk come down from the hamlets thronged the square right up to the
steps of the church. At ten o'clock the Regent's car, escorted by the Guard of Order, passed beneath
the arch of greenery raised at the entrance to the town; the bells rang; a schoolchild recited the
oath of the Order without a single mistake.

The Regent reviewed the garrison, greeted the veterans of the Passes and visited the forts along the
Corvenne road, from which he gazed for a long while down into the valley beyond the frontier, in
Thalberie. “We shall take nothing from anyone,” he told the officers. “We shall keep what is ours.”

### The Regent's Cup

In the afternoon, at the head office of the **Hautcorvin Glassworks**, the oldest firm in the province
offered its guest the masterpiece of its workshops. **Mrs Colombe Brossal**, daughter of the master
glassmaker, and **Mr Aimé Brossal**, company secretary, handed him the **Regent's Cup**: a footed
crystal cup, blown in the Vernezac hall and engraved with the Regent's profile within a laurel wreath
of which one can count, with a loupe, every vein. The Regent raised it towards the window to see the
work and said: “That is what a country does when it stands firm.” Mr Brossal brought the engraver
forward, who bowed without a word.

The Cup will join the collections of the Regency. The Glassworks, Mr Brossal tells us, will make a
series from it “for the homes of the Order”.

### Our photograph

In the great drawing room of the Glassworks, Mrs and Mr Brossal present the Cup to the Regent, seen
from behind. In the background, out of focus, near the window, a young man in a dark smock, his face
turned towards the Cup, whom Mr Brossal introduced to us as the engraver: “A. Dalvarin, from our
Vernezac workshops”.
