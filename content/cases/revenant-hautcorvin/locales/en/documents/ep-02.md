Black-and-white print, 13 × 18, white borders, no reference number. Arrived by post at office 12 of
the Office in September 1976, without a letter, in an envelope stamped at Vernezac; placed in the
closed file.

The doorstep of a stone hut, in full sun, with patches of summer snow in the scree. Seven people in
front of the door. In the centre, a woman in a man's shirt with the sleeves rolled up, short black
hair, a cowlick in the middle of the forehead, a horn whistle round her neck, laughs as she looks to
one side of the camera. On her right, a thin man with cropped white hair, black eyebrows, a linen suit
too wide for him, an engraver's loupe hanging from a cord; he is not laughing, but he is not looking
at his feet. Behind them, a very tall old man with ears that stick out, leaning on a stick, and a
young man with the same ears, glacier goggles pushed up on his forehead. On the left, a bearded
guide, badge No. 31 on his chest, carries his ice axe under his arm like an umbrella; a woman in
butterfly glasses takes his arm. A little apart, a bald man in a bow tie, who has kept his jacket on.

At the right-hand edge of the frame, far enough away that someone hesitated over keeping her in the
print, a white-haired woman sits on a stone by the path, lower down, a stick across her knees. She
is the only one in a black coat.
