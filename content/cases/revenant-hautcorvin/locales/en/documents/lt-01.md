*Letter from Honoré Tessonnal to Danela, two sheets of Glassworks writing paper with the letterhead
cut away with scissors. Black ink, a broad, heavy hand, three crossings-out. Folded in four, worn at
the folds. Found in the bundle returned to Honoré by a notary in Brénica in November 1960 (“letters
from Mr H. T. to Mrs D. D.”), then deposited at the Office with his papers.*

Hautcorvin, 2 July 1912

Danela,

I am writing to you what I cannot manage to say to you in the workshop, in front of the other two,
who listen to everything while pretending to turn the wheel.

Annette Neviot will come. I saw her on Sunday; she says it will be the end of August or the first
days of September, and that a girl from the Dalva who carried sacks of charcoal all through her
childhood needs nobody to help her give birth, but that she will come all the same. She will say
nothing. In thirty-eight years of practice she has never said anything about anybody: that is why
they send for her everywhere.

Léonce will be godfather: he is willing to carry that for me. He came back from Tennok in February
with a cold and stories about a woman keeper; when I told him, he laughed, then he stopped laughing,
then he said yes. You know what he is like. He says it costs him nothing, that he has no wife and no
chance of one, and that a godson is all a lens maker can hope for by way of posterity. Let him talk.
Marguerite will hold the child with him. She knows too; she did not say a word, she only looked at
me the way she used to when we were small and I had broken something.

My father will not have the child bear our name. ~~I could not~~ I did not know how to stand up to
him; you knew it before I did. He will bear yours, which is a fine one, and he will bear it better
than I bear mine.

The workshop will pay you what is needed every month. I have given the order to the cashier: it will
go in the engravers’ pay book, under your initials, and nobody will look. It is not a wage. Do not
send it back.

I do not know how to write these letters. I know how to make a cup. I will blow one for him when he
is here, and you will engrave it.
