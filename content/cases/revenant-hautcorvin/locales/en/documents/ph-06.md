## Front

Print, 18 × 24 cm, on pearl-grey card with a gilt rule; embossed in the corner: *Studio Orlhac —
Hautcorvin*. Three front steps before a door with a fanlight. Ten people.

In the centre, the bride and groom. She is about twenty: a short white satin dress, a veil low on
the forehead, very pale eyes, a dimple in her left cheek; a glass brooch engraved with a [[dove|colombe]] is
pinned at her shoulder. He, barely thirty, has a pencil moustache, hair slicked down with a parting,
a stiff collar and a white pocket handkerchief; his right eyebrow is raised, as if he had doubts
about the photographer.

To the bride’s left, a square-built man of about fifty with bushy eyebrows and a marked dimple, who
does not know what to do with his huge hands, seamed with burns; on his arm, a woman in a cloche
hat, a velvet ribbon close round her long neck, very pale grey eyes. To the groom’s right, an old
lady with white hair, a round brooch of gilded glass at her collar, and a nun in a white cornette,
a cross of blue glass on her breast, a dimple in her left cheek. At the end of the row, a tall lad
of about eighteen in his Sunday best, pale eyes, a lock of hair standing up in a cowlick in the
middle of his forehead, laughing with his mouth open. Three guests, a man and two women, close the
group on the groom’s side.
