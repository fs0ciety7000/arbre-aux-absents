**THE CORVINS SENTINEL** — Weekly of the upper valley — Hautcorvin — Saturday 13 June 1936 —
15 sous

## WALKOUT AT THE VERNEZAC FURNACES

### The glassblowers refuse the cut in wages; the management will not receive their delegates

No glass has been blown in the Vernezac hall since Monday. The furnaces have not been put out – one
does not put out a furnace like a lamp: the stokers keep them going, out of duty and because
rebuilding a melted furnace costs more than a walkout. But the blowpipes are racked, and the hundred
and forty men of the hall take turns in the yard, where the women of Vernezac bring them up their soup.

The origin of the dispute is well known. On 30 May, a notice posted in the hall announced, “in view of
the state of deposits”, a twelve per cent cut in wages from July. The Glassworks have not forgotten
the crash of 1929, which cost them their reserves; nor have the workers, who already gave back five
per cent in 1930.

The men who walked out have chosen three delegates. At their head is a man the hall has known since
childhood: **the glassblower Bastien Tessonnal**, twenty-six, **night foreman** at Furnace No. 2, son
of the master glassmaker Honoré Tessonnal. “You don't blow glass with promises,” he told us. “Show us
the books, and we'll blow.”

**Mr Aimé Brossal, company secretary** of the Glassworks, **refuses to receive the delegates** until
work has resumed. “The firm has no business negotiating with a factory yard,” he declared to us. “It
negotiates with its bankers, and I assure you that is quite enough.”

The master glassmaker, when asked, did not wish to take sides between his son-in-law and his son. He
was seen on Tuesday crossing the yard among the men, greeting each of them by name, and going up to
his furnace, where he spent the day alone.

### Our photograph

In front of the door of the hall, among the workers, a young man in a vest raises his arm. He has
pushed his cap back on his head; a tuft of hair stands up in the middle of his forehead, and, in the
newspaper's screen, his very pale eyes look white. He is shouting something, his mouth wide open.
