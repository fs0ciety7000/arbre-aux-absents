## Front

Gelatin silver print, 18 × 24 cm, fogged in the sky. A new stone hut with a stone-slab roof, the
door still pale; patches of snow linger among the scree, a flag hangs from the ridge. Above the
door, a painted board: *Refuge de l’Ourse — Vernezac Guides’ Company — 1911*. Some twenty people in
their Sunday clothes and hobnailed boots.

In the foreground, a priest of about fifty in a black cassock with white bands raises the
aspergillum towards the door. Near him, a heavily built old man with a horseshoe beard and a round
hat leans on a quarryman’s shovel; his left hand, resting on the handle, has only three fingers.
Beside him, a round-faced woman in a black bonnet tied under the chin, iron-framed spectacles, a
leather satchel slung across her and a medal pinned to her bodice.

A little further back, a man of about thirty with a thick moustache and a felt hat with a grouse
feather, a coiled rope slung across his chest; on his jacket, a badge bearing the number 7. He
holds by the hand a little boy of three in a bonnet. On his arm, a young woman with round cheeks,
her hair in a high chignon, a check apron over her Sunday dress, a bunch of keys at her belt.

Behind them, the Company’s guides, ropes over their shoulders, and children sitting on the low
wall.
