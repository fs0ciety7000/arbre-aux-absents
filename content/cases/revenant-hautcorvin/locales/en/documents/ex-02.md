::stamp[NATIONAL ARCHIVES — PALAIS DES CHARTES — CONSERVATION WORKSHOP]

**Comparative examination of handwriting and materials** — ORC file 76-0012

Aubrelieu, 16 February 1976

*Request from the rapporteur (office 12) of 10 February: to state whether the handwriting of the
documents below is that of one hand or of several. I am not a handwriting expert sworn to the courts;
I am a conservator, a woman who mends paper. I look at the paper, the ink, the movement of the hand.
I say what I see.*

## Documents examined

| Mark | Document | Date | Material, instrument |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Pencilled note signed “B.”, handed in by Mrs C. Brossal (item 11) | 1 Dec. 1937 | squared exercise-book paper, soft graphite pencil |
| B | Petition for re-registration, Form R-4, signed “Bastien Tessonnal” | 12 Jan. 1976 | printed form, blue ink, fine-nibbed pen |
| C | The claimant's signatures on records of hearing No. 1 and No. 2 | 13 Jan. and 5 Feb. 1976 | black ink, registry pen |
| D | Record of re-registration of Andrin Dalvarin, Vernezac (certified copy from the municipal archives): handwritten statement by the re-registered man, “The whole of the foregoing is true”, and his signature | 3 June 1953 | violet ink, school pen |
| E | Telegram lodging form, Vernezac post office, printed form No. 698, filled in by the sender (released on requisition) | 10 Jan. 1976 | black ballpoint pen, capitals |

No signed document from 1938 (customs record, restricted collection of the Census and Removals
Bureau) had been released to the workshop at the date of this examination.

## Observations

1. **The crossbar of the T.** In B, C, D and E, the crossbar of the capital T starts from the right,
   comes back to the left and closes in a loop onto the stem, before setting off towards the next
   letter. It is the model taught in Thalbère schools; I have met it in certificates from Brenzac.
   It can be seen in D in the T of “The” that opens the statement (the signature “Andrin Dalvarin”
   has no T and says nothing on this point), and in E in every “STOP” of the text, capitals
   included. In A, the T of “Tell”, which opens two sentences, is crossed with a straight bar,
   short, lifted, never joined: that is the way it is taught in the schools of the upper valley.
2. **The signature on B.** The T of “Tessonnal” was traced twice. Under the loop one can make out the
   start of a straight bar broken off a third of the way along, then covered over. The writer began a
   T with a straight bar, stopped, then closed it into a loop, as his hand is used to doing. The clerk
   notes in B that the claimant went back over the T twice: that is what this is.
3. **The rest of the hand.** B, C, D and E: an even slope to the right, equal pressure, letters
   detached in places the way one lifts the strokes of a drawing, no tremor. A: a variable slope,
   heavy pressure, quick joins, from a hand used to writing fast and little.
4. **Time.** Handwriting changes with age, illness, detention. It trembles, it slows, it simplifies.
   It does not change school.

## Conclusion

The writer of 1976 is the writer of 1953. The writer of 1937 is not the writer of 1976. Names are the
Office's business.
