**THE CORVINS SENTINEL** — Weekly of the upper valley — Hautcorvin — Saturday 4 December 1937
— 20 sous

*Passed by the Provincial Press Bureau.*

## THE VERNEZAC BLAZE: THE MASTER'S SON ON THE RUN

### The furnace hall destroyed in the night of Saturday to Sunday; the night foreman sought by the Guard of Order

Of the eastern half of the Vernezac furnace hall nothing remains but blackened walls and fallen
beams, and the smell, which could still be noticed on Thursday from the Hautcorvin road. The blaze
broke out on Saturday 27 November at about twenty to midnight. The pumps from Vernezac and those of
the Hautcorvin garrison fought it until dawn; they saved the furnaces, the engraving workshop and the
lodgings, and nothing else.

The night watchman, **Mr Toussaint Mallet**, sixty, was **badly burnt on the hands** while trying to
stop the fire single-handed. He was taken to the hospice, where it has not yet been possible to
question him.

The night foreman, on the other hand, has not been seen since. **The glassworker Bastien Tessonnal**,
the master glassmaker's son, whom our readers know as the ringleader of the walkout of June 1936, was
on duty that night; he left the hall before the pumps arrived and cannot be found. The Guard of
Order, which opened an inquiry on Sunday morning under the direction of Lieutenant Saunier, is
looking for him “to be questioned”. The lieutenant told us: “A man with nothing to reproach himself
with stays at his post. We shall find him.”

It does not take a scholar to see where this road leads. The ringleaders of 1936 promised fire to the
house that kept them; the fire has come.

### What the management says

**Mr Aimé Brossal**, company secretary of the Glassworks, kindly received us at the head office in
Hautcorvin. “I had left the hall at eight o'clock in the evening, after the Saturday pay,” he told us.
“I accuse no one: the Guard will do its work. I will say only that a furnace must be watched, by night
as by day. The Glassworks will rebuild. They are insured, and they have orders from the State.”

The master glassmaker, Mr Honoré Tessonnal, has made no statement.
