## [commun] The sitting

On Monday 15 March 1976, the Re-registration Commission sat in Aubrelieu on joined files
Nos 76-0012 and 74-0962. The table of descent held: twenty-eight slots, each resting on two
documents, and the claimant's slot was no longer in question. The report concluded that the
petition should be rejected and the impersonation reported. The Commission concurred.

The claimant contested nothing. Asked his name, he gave his own: Andrin Dalvarin, glass engraver,
prisoner number 1171. He asked only whether the young woman in the second petition would suffer for
it. The chairman replied that her petition would be examined on the documents, like any other.

Bastien Tessonnal was re-registered of the Commission's own motion, posthumously: died on Kessaar on
9 March 1944, prisoner number 0214. Petition No. 74-0962 was declared “not established”: Agnès
Neviot's parentage did not rest on two documents. Bastien's share in the estate of Honoré
Tessonnal, for want of an established descendant, passed to the other heirs.

On 26 April, the Aubrelieu criminal court tried the impersonation. The accused pleaded guilty. He
was given a suspended sentence of six months' imprisonment, “in view of the circumstances”.

## [s1] The Fire of 1937

To the first question the report answered with a name, and this time with documents to back it:
Aimé Brossal, company secretary of the Glassworks, died 1958. It cited the report of the L'Égide
assessor, which placed the seat of the fire far from the furnaces, and the log of Toussaint Mallet,
night watchman, whose torn-out page had left its trace pressed into the next one: “23.10 — M. B. at
the packing store”.

Colombe Brossal had not appeared in February, when her envelope was opened. She came to the
sitting. The chairman read out the inventory of the 1958 envelope: Rider No. 3 to the L'Égide
policy, two petrol chits initialled “A. B.”, a receipt from the Vèze Garage dated 26 November 1937,
Mallet's letter. When he had finished, she stood, identified each item as one she had put under
seal herself, and said: “My brother did not set the fire. I have known it for eighteen years.” She
sat down again and did not look at the claimant's bench.

The note was entered in the margin of her brother's re-registration: “died on Kessaar on 9 March
1944, innocent”.

## [s2] The Pas de l'Ourse

In July, a plaque of engraved glass was set into the stone to the right of the door of the Refuge de
l'Ourse. It had come from Brénica by post, carefully packed, with no letter. Victorin Sarrade fixed
it himself, with four brass screws, and did not ask where it had come from:

> Through this pass, from 1938 to 1952, Lucie and Jules Neviot brought one hundred and forty-two of
> the absent across.

## [s3] Honoré's envelope

The report also established that Andrin Dalvarin was the son of Honoré Tessonnal. The sealed
envelope of the will, addressed “to whichever of my children did not bear my name”, therefore had a
recipient. Maître Escande sent it by registered post to Brénica. The acknowledgement of receipt came
back three weeks later, signed “A. Dalvarin” with a very careful D. Of what the envelope held, the
office keeps only a copy, made at the recipient's request:

> Andrin,
>
> If Maître Escande gives you this, the Office has spoken, and I shall have been wrong to keep silent
> to the very end. I never knew how to call you “my son” except in glass. I said it in florins, in
> the workshop's pay book, from 1912 to 1938, and your mother was kind enough to pretend she did not
> understand.
>
> Léonce carried what I did not carry. He was your godfather, he let people talk, and he died
> without once complaining of it.
>
> In 1952 it was through you that I learned of Bastien's death. You did not know whom you were
> telling. I did not reply. I wrote “to my living children”, and it was you I was thinking of; I
> could not bring myself to write your name anywhere but here.
>
> Honoré Tessonnal

He had had no need to lie. He was an heir under his own name. Maître Escande wrote to him twice about
the estate. He did not reply, then replied in a single line: “Set aside a share for the girl. You
know which one.”

## [s4] The Regent's Cup

The Regent's Cup left the exhibits store and entered the Museum of the Fire Arts in Aubrelieu, with
a new label:

> Andrin Dalvarin, 1941. In the laurel: B · T · 38.

## [conclusion] Conclusion

In the register, three notes: “Re-registered posthumously, 1976”; “Petition rejected, 1976”;
“Parentage not established”.

Andrin Dalvarin left the day after the judgment, by the road over the pass, with one suitcase and
the suit that was too big for him. He did not go by way of Vernezac.

A week later, Agnès Neviot received at the hospice a parcel posted from Brénica, with no sender's
name. Inside was a wheel-engraved glass: the Pas de l'Ourse, three bends of the path, a she-bear
lying beneath the gap. The one from 1959 had borne a date. This one bore none.
