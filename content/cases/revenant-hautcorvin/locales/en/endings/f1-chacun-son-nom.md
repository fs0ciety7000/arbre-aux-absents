## [commun] The sitting

On Monday 15 March 1976, the Re-registration Commission sat in Aubrelieu on joined files
Nos 76-0012 and 74-0962. The table of descent held: twenty-eight slots, each resting on two
documents, and the claimant's slot was no longer in question. The report proposed to restore
everyone. The Commission concurred.

Bastien Tessonnal, glassblower, born in Hautcorvin in 1910, was re-registered posthumously: died
on Kessaar on 9 March 1944, prisoner number 0214. Agnès Neviot, born in Vernezac on 14 February
1939, was acknowledged as his daughter; under Article 12, she would represent her father in the
estate of Honoré Tessonnal. The claimant was entered under the name he had left in the cloakroom in
January: Andrin Dalvarin, glass engraver, prisoner number 1171, son of Danela Dalvarin.

The chairman asked him whether he had anything to add. He answered: “No. She might.” Agnès added
nothing. She had kept her uniform on under her coat; she was on duty at six.

The Commission referred nothing to the public prosecutor. The report did not invite it to, and the
chairman remarked, for the record, that a man who borrows a dead man's name to give it back to his
daughter has taken nothing from anyone.

## [s1] The Fire of 1937

To the first question the report answered with a name, and this time with documents to back it:
Aimé Brossal, company secretary of the Glassworks, died 1958. It cited the report of the L'Égide
assessor, which placed the seat of the fire far from the furnaces, and the log of Toussaint Mallet,
night watchman, whose torn-out page had left its trace pressed into the next one: “23.10 — M. B. at
the packing store”.

Colombe Brossal had not appeared in February, when her envelope was opened; she had sent word that
she opposed nothing. She came to the sitting. The chairman read out the inventory of the 1958
envelope: Rider No. 3 to the L'Égide policy, two petrol chits initialled “A. B.”, a receipt from the
Vèze Garage dated 26 November 1937, Mallet's letter. When he had finished, she stood and identified
each item as one she had put under seal herself. Then she said: “My brother did not set the fire. I
have known it for eighteen years.” She sat down again, very upright, and looked at no one.

The note was entered in the margin of the re-registration: “died on Kessaar on 9 March 1944,
innocent”.

## [s2] The Pas de l'Ourse

In July, a plaque of engraved glass was set into the stone to the right of the door of the Refuge de
l'Ourse. Andrin had engraved it in Brénica, on the wheel, over three evenings. Victorin Sarrade fixed
it himself, with four brass screws and without anyone's help, because it was his place to do it:

> Through this pass, from 1938 to 1952, Lucie and Jules Neviot brought one hundred and forty-two of
> the absent across.

There is no customs officer's name on the plaque. He did not ask for one.

## [s3] Honoré's envelope

The report also established that Andrin Dalvarin was the son of Honoré Tessonnal, and the Commission
entered him as such. The sealed envelope of the will was addressed “to whichever of my children did
not bear my name”; it now had a recipient. Maître Escande opened it the next day, in his office, and
handed it to Andrin, who read it standing up.

> Andrin,
>
> If Maître Escande gives you this, the Office has spoken, and I shall have been wrong to keep silent
> to the very end. I never knew how to call you “my son” except in glass. I said it in florins, in
> the workshop's pay book, from 1912 to 1938, and your mother was kind enough to pretend she did not
> understand.
>
> Léonce carried what I did not carry. He was your godfather, he let people talk, and he died
> without once complaining of it.
>
> In 1952 it was through you that I learned of Bastien's death. You did not know whom you were
> telling. I did not reply. I wrote “to my living children”, and it was you I was thinking of; I
> could not bring myself to write your name anywhere but here.
>
> Honoré Tessonnal

He had had no need to lie. He inherited under his own name, on an equal footing with Colombe.

## [s4] The Regent's Cup

The Regent's Cup left the exhibits store, where it had lain under seal since 1941, and entered the
Museum of the Fire Arts in Aubrelieu. Mathias Orvane had the label rewritten and a magnifying glass
fitted to the case, at a child's height:

> Andrin Dalvarin, 1941. In the laurel: B · T · 38.

## [conclusion] Conclusion

In the register, three notes: “Re-registered posthumously, 1976”; “Restored under his own name,
1976”; “Parentage established, 1976”. Each fits on a single line.

On Saturday 10 July, Andrin and Agnès walked up together to the Refuge de l'Ourse, by the path he
knew stone by stone without ever having taken it with the man who had described it to him. She went
first. At the last bend she turned round to tell him he could stop for breath. He said no, he was
used to it, and sat down all the same.
