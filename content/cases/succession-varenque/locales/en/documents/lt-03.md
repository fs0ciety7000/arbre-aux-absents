*Drawing paper torn from a sketchbook, a broad, slanting hand, a Prussian-blue thumbprint in the
corner.*

Aubrelieu, 12 rue des Tanneurs, Tuesday 15 May 1906

Adé, old girl,

Sit down, or rather don’t, stay on your feet, you look handsomer standing up when you put on your
schoolteacher’s face.

Pauline has had a son, Victor, on the 12th, in the small hours, while the rue des Tanneurs was
still asleep and I was not asleep at all. He is mine and he squints like me: the left eye, the very
same, the one that wanders off to see what’s going on elsewhere. The neighbour who delivered him
says it will pass. It never passed for me.

Augustin won’t hear of it. I went to Moulinvert on Sunday to tell him to his face. He received me
in his Deputy’s study, beneath his own election portrait, and explained to me that a Deputy for the
Orvelle had no nephew in the rue des Tanneurs, that elections are lost for less, and that if I cared
for the family I had only to hold my tongue. He said “family” the way one says “trading name”.

So the child was registered under his mother’s name, by a neighbour, with “father not named” at the
end of the line. I was not named. And yet I feel thoroughly named: I am his father, and I shall be
his father even if nobody writes it down.

Don’t tell Mama. Or do tell her: she will draw a fern and say nothing, which is what she does best.

Your brother, who sends you a kiss and hasn’t a sou left,

::signature[Lucien]
