## VEL TAALIK — the Messenger of the Sea — No. 1941 — October 1936

### The women who welcome: the midwives of the coast

*Third article in our series “The trades of the coast”.*

In Vellaak, when someone knocks at a door in the night, it is not always for a misfortune. There is
a woman whom people come to ask to get up, take her bag and her lantern, and walk a league in the
rain to a farm on the moor. She goes. She comes back in the morning with one more birth in the
commune register and, as she puts it, “one pair of boots fewer”.

She is simply called Hessa. All Vellaak calls her that; half of Vellaak, it is true, owes her its
birth. A midwife, qualified at the Aubrelieu school in 1927, she has to date brought four hundred
and eleven children into the world between the cape and the head of the fjord, including twins on
two occasions and a boy in the ferryman’s boat, in the very middle of the water, “which is not
recommended”.

She asks us to print three things. That there must be boiled water, always. That fathers must wait
outside, always. And that her own son gave her more trouble than the other four hundred and ten put
together.

### Photograph

A moorland path above the sea, the grass flattened by the wind. A woman of about thirty walks
towards the lens: broad face, braids pinned up in a tight crown, a dark birthmark on her neck, on
the right side; a big leather bag slung across her and, in her hand, an unlit lantern.
