## The Velmarche Eye — No. 1844 — 20 November 1937 — 30 sous

### OUR FINE INDUSTRIES — THE PAPER TRADES

**At Moulinvert, on the Clairette, the Velmarchois sheet is still born by hand.**

We think we know paper: we buy it, we write on it, we throw it away. We do not know that it was
once water, rags and patience. Our correspondent spent a day at the Varenque Paper Mills, supplier
to the great departments of state since the gold medal of 1889, where three hundred working men and
women carry on a craft of which Velmarche may be proud, today more than ever.

There is the vat, where the vatman lifts the mould in a single movement and the pulp becomes a
sheet. There is the drying loft, a long nave where the sheets hang like washing. There is the ream
room, where they count by hand up to five hundred without ever going wrong, or so we are assured.

And there is, at the far end of a glazed workshop that smells of acid and metal, the most discreet
and most secret trade in the house: that of **watermark engraver**.

It is she who engraves and shapes, with burin and brass wire, the designs you see in the sheet when
you hold it up to the lamp: the fern that made the firm’s name, the arms of the ministries, the
ciphers of the banks. “A watermark is a signature that no one can forge,” she tells us, “provided
the engraver is honest.” She laughs. The back of her right hand still bears the bite of the acid.

Miss **Odile Varenque**, granddaughter of the founder, learnt the trade at twenty-three from the
firm’s old mould-cutter, whose place she has taken. She works alone. She does not like being
photographed; she gave way for *The Velmarche Eye* on the express condition that the mould be shown
as well.

## Main photograph

A young woman of about thirty, bent over a workbench under a lamp with a metal shade. Straight
brown hair cut at the chin, a white forelock above the forehead on the left, thick eyebrows knitted
in concentration. Workshop smock with the sleeves rolled up. In her left hand she holds a
papermaker’s mould strung with brass wires; in her right, a burin. On the back of that hand, a pale,
shiny patch: a burn scar.

Caption: *Miss O. Varenque, watermark engraver, at the Moulinvert Paper Mills.*
