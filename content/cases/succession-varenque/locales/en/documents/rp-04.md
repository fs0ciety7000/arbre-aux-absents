## COMMISSION OF INQUIRY INTO THE REGENCY’S WARDS’ CHARITY

### Second report: the blaze at the Grisenoue home (9 April 1952) and the fate of the registers

*Chairman: Councillor Bertrand Ollivry. Rapporteurs: Messrs Lebas and Saint-Aubin, Mrs Jeanne Cassaigne (rapporteur 3).*

**1. The facts.** On 9 April 1952, the third day of the events known as the “April Days”, the Grisenoue home of the **Wards’** Charity, which then housed one hundred and eighty-seven children, was evacuated at seven o’clock in the morning on the orders of its director. At ten o’clock the archives building, then the administrative wing, were set alight with petrol. The children, gathered in the chapel, came to no harm. The fire, fanned by the wind off the marshes, spread to the roofs; the Grisenoue firemen, who arrived towards noon, could save only the dormitory block.

**2. The order.** The Commission heard the director of the home, the bursar and four matrons. Their evidence agrees. The **blaze** was started in execution of a **written order**, brought from the Citadel at Aubrelieu at dawn on 9 April by a dispatch rider of the Guard of Order, and worded as follows: “Destroy without delay all the admission, placement and correspondence registers of the home. No document is to survive.” The order was signed “**M. Daubry**, Commissioner of Registers for the province”. The original has not been found: the director states that he threw it into the fire himself, in accordance with a note at the foot of the order. Miss Rose Pellerin, matron, states that she read it and recognises the signature, “which she saw every month at the foot of the placement orders”.

**3. The author of the order.** Mr Marcel **Daubry**, Commissioner of Registers for the province of the Orvelle from 1938 to 1952, left Aubrelieu in the night of 9 to 10 April 1952. He is believed to have crossed into Thalberie by the Col de Corvenne. He has not answered the Commission’s summonses. A warrant for his arrest was issued on 2 June 1953.

**4. What was saved.** Contrary to what was at first believed, the blaze did not destroy everything. A Grisenoue fireman, Mr Léon Hébrard, recovered from the rubble of the archives building, on 10 April, a bundle of half-burnt leaves belonging to the **admissions register for the year 1942**, which he handed in at the town hall. These leaves were placed under seal by the Commission (**seal No. 7, leaves 212–213**) and are now kept at the National Archives with the Commission’s collection. They cover the admissions numbered P-4468 to P-4473.

**5. An anomaly.** The Commission notes that one of these leaves, file **P-4471** (a girl born on 3 March 1942), records a placement “by order of the commissioner”, ordered by name and without an observation period. Of the three hundred or so placements for the year 1942 that the Commission has been able to reconstruct from other sources, it is the only one to bear such a note. The name of the receiving family, written at the edge of the leaf, has burnt away; only the initials “Mr and Mrs M. D.” remain. The Commission has not been able to identify it with certainty.

**6. Conclusions.** The Commission considers it proven that the destruction of the Grisenoue registers was ordered by Commissioner Daubry with the aim of preventing the identification of the children placed and of their parents. It recommends that the leaves saved be released, on request by name, to the persons named in them and to their families, once a law allows it.
