*A sheet of bluish paper, written in soft pencil, in Ostrian, in a careful hand that forms the
letters one by one. A half-sheet from a school exercise book is pinned over it: the translation, in
ink, in a schoolgirl’s hand, added years later.*

## Address

To Captain Firmin Varenque, aboard the *Orvelle*, Orvelle Packet Company, Port-Sénane — please
forward.

::stamp[VELMARCHE POSTS — TENNOK — 14 MARCH 1883]

## The letter

*Firmin, mo kaari —*

*Isa Joren ookta, tenn-laak, sel sanni sui. Tenn ei ookta : mo tenn kaamen. Holmen ta naaden, mo
tenn kaamen.*

*Velmarke-mennes, Lights and Beacons, mo nimme tenn-vaardessa. Mo, Maaren. Aldrik ommen tenn-vaard
sel ; mo saar riinen : ees ta, vaan ta.*

*Tule. Ei tule, vel kaamen tu. Mo tenn kaamen tu.*

::signature[Maaren Tennokessa]

## The pinned translation

::hand[Translation of Aunt Maaren’s letter, done by me, Adé, at Tennok, in the summer of 1893, for Uncle Firmin, who speaks Ostrian but can’t read it, because nobody learnt it at school.]

Firmin, my dear –

Father Joren is dead, by the fire, on the night of the snow. The fire has not died: I am the one
who holds it. Day and night, I hold it.

The gentlemen of Velmarche, Lights and Beacons, have appointed me woman keeper of the light.
*Tenn-vaardessa*: she made the word on purpose, because it did not exist. Me, Maaren. Aldrik says
he will be keeper after me; I told him first to learn to hold his tongue, and to count.

Come back. Do not come back, if the sea holds you. I hold the fire for you.

Maaren Tennokessa

::hand[Uncle said it was right, except “my dear”: in Ostrian it is stronger, but he would not tell me how much stronger.]
