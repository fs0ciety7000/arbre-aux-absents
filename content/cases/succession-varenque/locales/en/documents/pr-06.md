**THE VELMARCHE EYE** — Illustrated weekly — No. 1395 — Saturday 16 February 1929 — 25 sous

## THE THEATRES

### Blanche Aubertine triumphs in *The Lantern of Ostrie*

*Seventeen curtain calls last Saturday at the Aubrelieu Opera for the first performance of Mr Aurèle Vasquin’s new work. A soprano is born; she had, it seems, someone to take after.*

It is a long while since such a thing was seen beneath the chandelier of the **Aubrelieu Opera**: a house on its feet, stalls forgetting their gloves, a tenor stepping aside with a smile to let his partner through at the seventeenth curtain call. *The Lantern of Ostrie*, a lyric drama in three acts by **Mr Aurèle Vasquin**, to a libretto by Mr Gérard Hollain, was given its first performance on Saturday 9 February and, let us say it at once, it will be the event of the season. It may be the event of the decade.

The work, as is known, brings to the stage a legend of the north coast: the daughter of an Ostrian lighthouse keeper lights a lantern on the water every night for seven years for a betrothed the sea has not given back, until the night the lantern drifts back to the shore alone. Mr Vasquin, who spent two summers at Vellaak collecting fishermen’s songs, had the good taste to “arrange” nothing: the women’s chorus in the second act, sung in Ostrian, sent through the house a shiver the Opera’s regulars had never known.

### A voice

But it is Elva, the keeper’s daughter, whom all Aubrelieu is running to hear, and Elva is **Mrs Blanche Aubertine**. The young **soprano** was known to us, applauded since 1924 in parts where she had little to do but appear and die gracefully. No one suspected she could carry three acts, and a lantern aria in the third which demands at once the breath of a singer with twenty years on the stage and the freshness of a beginner. She has both. The top C at the close, taken *piano* and swelled into a cry, was greeted with an ovation the conductor had to halt with his baton in order to finish the work.

Our readers ask us who she is. Mrs Blanche Aubertine, as her stage name hints a little, was **née Varenque**: she is the **daughter of the former Deputy** for the Orvelle, Mr Augustin Varenque, now chairman of the Moulinvert Paper Mills, and the granddaughter, through her father’s mother, of the Aubertots of Aubrelieu. She studied singing at the Aubrelieu Conservatoire, against the wishes, it is said, of part of her family, which would have preferred for her a less public voice. On Saturday the family filled two boxes. It applauded like everyone else.
