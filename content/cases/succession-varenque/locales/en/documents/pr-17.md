**THE ORVELLE GAZETTE** — Moulinvert — Tuesday 7 September 1971 — 0.35 Fn

## DEATHS

### Gaspard Varenque is no more

*The former Director of the Paper Mills passed away on Thursday at Les Tilleuls, aged seventy-two. Moulinvert followed his coffin in silence.*

Mr **Gaspard Varenque** died on Thursday 2 September, in his seventy-third year, at his house, Les Tilleuls, where he had lived in retirement for eleven years. With him goes the last of the men who ran the **Varenque Paper Mills** in the old way, from the laboratory to the vat room, in a grey smock and without ever raising his voice.

Born at Moulinvert in 1898, grandson of the founder, Mr Anselme Varenque, and son of Mr Augustin Varenque, the former Deputy for the Orvelle, he entered the Paper Mills as an engineer straight from the Hautcorvin school of papermaking. He was their **director from 1925 to 1960**, and chairman from 1938. To him the house owes the modernisation of its machines, the apprentices’ mill in the rue Basse and the electrification of the wheels on the Clairette; it was under his direction that it passed through the darkest years of our history, which earned him, in 1953, a trial from which he emerged acquitted and a reputation he never chose to defend himself against. We shall not return to those years here. He did not return to them himself.

In 1922 he had married Miss Irène Castagne, the pianist, taken from us in 1945. His elder son, **Augustin**, a student, was shot by the Regency in 1944. He leaves **Maître Armand Varenque**, barrister, who succeeded him in 1960 as chairman of the board of the Paper Mills, and **Dr Hélène Marsal**, a paediatrician at Port-Sénane, as well as his sister, Mrs Blanche Daubry, and his aunt, Miss Adélaïde Varenque, ninety-one, who insisted on following the procession as far as the vault.

The funeral was held on Saturday in the church at Moulinvert, in the strictest privacy, as the deceased had wished. The workers of the Paper Mills, who had stopped the machines for an hour, lined the road to the cemetery. No speech was made. Mr Varenque, it is said, had left this one instruction: “No phrases. Blank paper.”

As he wished, the announcement bore no mention, and the slab of the vault will bear only his name and his dates.
