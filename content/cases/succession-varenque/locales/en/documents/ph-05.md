## Front

Large sepia print, slightly faded, grey card with a gilt border, blind stamp “Studio Lumen —
Aubrelieu” at the bottom right. The front steps of Les Tilleuls, two lime trees in flower,
wisteria along the balustrade. Late-morning light.

**Back row, standing, left to right:**

- a very thin man of about twenty-five, fine moustache, hair parted on the left, heavy eyelids,
  morning coat; a signet ring on his right hand, which rests on his lapel; turned three-quarters,
  he shows a small nick in the rim of his right ear;
- a young woman of about twenty in a pale drop-waisted dress, a string of pearls, short wavy hair,
  a beauty spot above the lip, on the left; she stands very close to the man before her;
- a stout man of about fifty, grey waxed handlebar moustache, pince-nez on a black ribbon, frock
  coat, a badge in his buttonhole;
- the bridegroom, in his twenties: morning coat, white carnation, round tortoiseshell spectacles,
  cleft chin, hair slicked down with a centre parting; his hand rests on the bride’s shoulder;
- a man in his fifties with white side-whiskers, double-breasted waistcoat, watch on a long chain;
- a little apart, at the end of the row, a sunburnt young man of about twenty, clean-shaven, in a
  suit too short at the cuffs, a gold ring in his left ear, his cap in his hand.

**Front row, seated, left to right:**

- a woman of about forty with dark hair in a chignon, a white forelock on the left, a beauty spot
  on her right cheek, a fob watch pinned to a dark dress;
- a very tall woman of about forty, greying chignon, feathered hat, a lorgnette on her lap, a dimple
  in her chin;
- the bride, barely twenty: short veil, black hair in a bob, long neck, a long jade necklace;
- an old lady with white hair, a knob-headed cane, a fern-shaped brooch at her collar.

**On the ground, in front, centre left:** a girl of about thirteen, sitting cross-legged in a
crumpled white dress, two plaits, a sharp white forelock above the forehead on the left, thick
eyebrows, an ink stain on one finger. She alone is not looking at the lens: she is looking at the
camera itself, as though she wanted to know how it works.
