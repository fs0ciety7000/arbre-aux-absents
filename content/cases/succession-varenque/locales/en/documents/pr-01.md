**THE AUBRELIEU COURIER** — Daily — Tuesday 22 October 1889 — Price per copy: 5 sous

## THE GRAND EXHIBITION OF AUBRELIEU

### The prize list is proclaimed: Velmarche discovers it is modern

On Sunday, beneath the great glass roof of the Exhibition palace, in the presence of His Highness the Grand Duke, of the Ministers and of four thousand people who did not all find a seat, the president of the general jury, Mr Léonce Hardouin, proclaimed the awards of the Grand Exhibition of Aubrelieu. The Exhibition, as our readers know, will close its doors at the end of the month, having received, from 5 May to this day, more than a million and a half visitors: a figure no one dared hope for in the spring, when the sour-minded were predicting that the glass roof would not survive the first storm.

It survived. And Velmarche, which had doubted itself, may today count its medals.

### Glass, soap and ships

In the class of the glass industries, the gold medal goes to the **Hautcorvin Glassworks**, for their great first-order lighthouse lens, so much admired by visitors at the centre of the Machine Gallery, and of which the jury said that it “would carry the light of Aubrelieu as far as the Mer Grise”. The **Mérande Soapworks**, of Bains-de-Mérande, receives a silver medal for its toilet soaps and for the grace of its labels. The **Orvelle Packet Company** wins a gold medal for its one-fiftieth-scale model of its new liner, the *Duchesse-d’Ambre*. **Studio Lumen** is rewarded for its permanent carbon portraits, and the **Belaube Department Store** receives an honourable mention for its pavilion, where, it is said, more umbrellas were sold than works of art.

### Fern paper

But the surprise of the day came from Class XII, paper and the book trades, where the gold medal, which everyone had promised to the great houses of the capital, was awarded to **Mr Anselme Varenque, papermaker at Moulinvert**, for his “fern paper”.

Those of our readers who visited the stand of the Varenque Paper Mills, at the far end of the Book Gallery, will doubtless remember the little apparatus at which anyone could hold a sheet up to a lamp: there appeared, in the very thickness of the paper, a frond of fern so fine that one could count its veins. The watermark was made **from drawings by Mrs Varenque, a distinguished botanist**, daughter of Mr Hippolyte Aubertot, the pharmacist of the rue des Halles, under whose guidance more than one student of Aubrelieu has gone out botanising.

The jury’s report notes that the Moulinvert house, “founded barely twenty years ago in an old grain mill on the Clairette”, now supplies a paper “that does not yellow, does not drink the ink and does not allow itself to be forged”. It was this last merit that decided the vote: the administration of the Velmarche Posts, we are assured, is already taking an interest.

Questioned on his way out, Mr Varenque, a man of few words, said only: “I don’t make speeches. I make paper.” Then he handed his medal to Mrs Varenque, adding: “It’s hers.” The company applauded.

### The other awards

The full list of medals, mentions and diplomas will appear in our Thursday supplement, at the usual price.
