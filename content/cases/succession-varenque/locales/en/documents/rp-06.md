## EXPERT REPORT

### Examination of the watermarks of four groups of papers — Request from the Civil Re-registration Office of 9 October 1974

Aubrelieu, 14 October 1974.

**Request.** The rapporteur in charge of file 74-0318 (the Adélaïde Varenque succession) has asked the conservation workshop to determine whether certain papers in the Varenque collection and in the holdings of the National Archives have a common origin.

**Method.** Examination in transmitted light and in raking light, contact tracing of the watermarks on film, measurement of the spacing of the laid lines and chain lines, comparison of mould defects. The original papers have undergone no intervention of any kind.

### 1. The commercial fern (reference)

Since 1889, every sheet sold by the Varenque Paper Mills as “fern paper” has carried, beneath the stem of the frond, a **year mark** (the year of manufacture, in figures five millimetres high) followed by the initials “PV”. The year-marked moulds are renewed every year and destroyed the year after. I have examined forty-eight reference sheets (1889 to 1971), including the paper of the **grey booklets** (fern year-marked 1938 to 1952). All carry the year mark and the initials.

### 2. The fern without a year mark

There exists another fern watermark: the same design, the same fineness, but **with neither year mark nor initials**: beneath the stem, the paper is blank. It can also be recognised by a constant engraving flaw, the **third pinnule on the left of the stem, broken**, which makes it possible to state that all the sheets bearing it come from the same mould, or from two twin moulds engraved by the same hand. This paper was **never sold**: it appears in no catalogue, no invoice and no sample book of the firm.

So far, I have found it in three groups only:

1. **The false papers of the Fern network, 1944–1952** (National Archives, collection of documents seized and documents handed in after 1952, reserve; one hundred and twelve booklets and cards examined). The forgeries seized in 1941 at the moulin de la Sorbe bear a different fern, engraved by hand on a makeshift mould.
2. **The letters signed “N.”** in the correspondence of Adélaïde Varenque (Varenque collection, art. 57), including the letter of 1962. The paper is identical to that of the booklets: same mould, same flaw, same absence of a date.
3. **The register with the blue cover** (Varenque collection, art. 41), according to my inventory notes of June 1974: its endpapers bear the same fern with no year mark. As the original is now missing, I have been unable to repeat the examination; security microfilm MF-74-112 does not reproduce watermarks. The register also records, from July 1944 to April 1952, outgoings of paper entered under the heading “**undated fern**”.

### Conclusions

- The “undated fern” of the blue register and the fern without a year mark of the network’s false papers (1944–1952) designate, in all likelihood, **the same paper**.
- This paper was made at the Varenque Paper Mills, on a mould that was never used for commercial production.
- The person who wrote the letters signed “N.”, until 1962 at least, had access to this paper.

I express no opinion on the identity of the persons concerned.

**Observation.** I must point out to the Office that I myself drew up the inventory of the Varenque collection, that I bear the name of a person cited in the file, and that I am the adopted daughter of Mrs Blanche Daubry, née Varenque. I know of no blood tie between myself and that family. I leave it to the Office to judge whether this examination should be entrusted to another workshop.
