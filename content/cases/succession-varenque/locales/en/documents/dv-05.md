## A SHORT OSTRIAN–VELMARCHOIS LEXICON

### *Taalikmaal* — The words of the coast, for those who have lost them and those who never had them

By **Selda Varenque**, editor of *Vel Taalik*. — Vellaak, *Vel Taalik* press, 1954. — 64 pages, 4 Fn.

**Handwritten inscription, on the flyleaf:**

::hand[For Adé, who was the first to translate grandmother Maaren's letters, and who has never needed this book. With the love of the whole coast. — Selda, Vellaak, June 1954.]

---

### Foreword

For fifteen years they burnt our registers, shut down our papers and punished our children for a word. This little book does not claim to give back what was taken. It only gives those who read our names on maps, on graves and on certificates the means to understand what they are reading. For with us, a name always says something: a place, a creature, the weather that day.

Since 1898 Ostrian has been written in the official spelling: it has no *c*, no *q*, no *ph*, no *x*; long vowels are doubled (*aa*, *ee*); the stress always falls on the first syllable.

### II. The grammar of names

**-essa**: marks the feminine of family names. *Tennok* gives *Tennokessa*, “she of Tennok”; *Orrvik* gives *Orrvikessa*; *Draavik*, *Draavikessa*. In old names and in poetry, the ending is often shortened to **-ess**. Velmarchois registers drop the suffix: *Maaren Tennokessa* becomes *Maaren Tennok* in them. With us, women keep their own name after marriage.

**Ta** joins two names on marriage certificates, photographs and graves: *Hessa Draavikessa ta Firmin Varenque*.

### IV. Nicknames of the coast

On the coast, boat skippers are given the name of a bird or a beast, which the whole harbour uses and the registers ignore. *Maask*, the Gull, is said of a man who always comes home; *Haask*, the Falcon, of a man who sees a long way off. The nickname sometimes passes to the son, never to the register.

### V. Given names

*Maaren*, “she of the calm sea”; *Aldrik*, “old path”; *Tenna*, “little light”; *Selda*, “she who keeps”; *Hessa*, “the gentle one”; *Orrin*, “son of the wind”; *Tavik*, “little bay”; *Joren*, “the watchman”; *Nora*, “the new one”, a rare name, given to girls born after a bereavement or to women who start again.
