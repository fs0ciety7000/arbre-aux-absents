**THE HERALD** — Organ of Velmarchois Order — Saturday 21 May 1938 — 20 sous

## THE NEW GREY BOOKLET: VARENQUE PAPER, VELMARCHOIS ORDER

### Inauguration in Aubrelieu of the Registers Office for the province of the Orvelle

*“A country that knows who it is has nothing to fear from anyone.” — The Regent, speech of 2 February.*

Order can be read. From now on it is read on Velmarchois paper, made by Velmarchois hands, in a Velmarchois valley. So the notables gathered on Thursday in the restored salons of the former Court of Audit were able to see for themselves, where the **Census and Removals Bureau** (**BRR**) is henceforth housing the Registers Office for the province of the Orvelle. The flag of Order flew on the façade; the Guard of Order presented arms; a large and disciplined crowd filled the square as far as the bridge.

The mission of this Office is well known, laid down by the Act of 2 February on the purity of the registers: to keep up to date, commune by commune, the civil registration of the Nation; to enter in it those who are worthy; to strike off those who, by their deeds, their words or their allegiance, have chosen to cut themselves off from it. What Velmarche tolerated through a century of parliamentary disorder it will tolerate no longer: every Velmarchois will have his page, and every page will tell the truth.

### The grey booklet

For persons whose conduct calls for particular attention, the Office will issue the new **grey booklet**, a control book which the bearer presents on every demand and in which the authorities record his movements, his acquaintances and his visas. It is printed on a special paper, pearl grey, proof against forgery, bearing as its watermark the frond of fern which, in 1889, brought glory to our industries at the Grand Exhibition.

This paper comes from the **Varenque Paper Mills**, of Moulinvert, which have received the order for the first printing: four hundred thousand booklets. “For seventy years we have made a paper that cannot be forged,” **Mr Gaspard Varenque, Director of the Paper Mills**, told *The Herald*. “The State asks of us what we know how to do. We do it.” Mr Varenque, as is known, took the chairmanship of the house in February, on the death of his father, the former Deputy, who had always had, he tells us, “a taste for well-kept papers”.

### The Commissioner of Registers

The provincial Registers Office is headed by **Mr Marcel Daubry, Commissioner of Registers**, who on Thursday received from the hands of the Minister of the Interior the grey sash of his office. Born at Saint-Ormel, the son of a court clerk, long attached to the administration of the Moulinvert Paper Mills, Mr Daubry knows the registers as other men know their breviary. “A name badly written,” he declared in a much-applauded address, “is a door badly shut. We shall shut the doors.”

Our readers will learn without surprise that the Commissioner and the papermaker are brothers-in-law: in 1923 Mr Daubry married a sister of Mr Varenque. There could be no better way of saying that Order is also a family affair.
