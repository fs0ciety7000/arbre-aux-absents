*Mauve paper scented with iris, a large round hand that takes up the whole page. A great many
exclamation marks, several of them added afterwards.*

Aubrelieu, 8 avenue de la Charte, 24 June 1942

My dear Adé,

Sit down. There: I am a mother!

On Saturday evening Marcel brought home a baby girl from Grisenoue, in a wicker basket, like an
Easter pie. He says she has nobody. He says it was the Charity that entrusted her to him, that
everything is in order and the papers will follow – you know what he is like about papers. He
doesn’t want questions asked. I ask none: I sing.

I am calling her Lise. Lise Daubry: say it out loud, it sounds like a poster for the Opera.

She is three months old, or a little more, has a boxer’s fists, and only cries during my vocal
exercises, which I take as musical criticism. And to think that for twenty years I believed I would
never have anything of my own but bouquets!

Don’t tell Mama, I want to tell her myself, with the little one in my arms, so that she can’t put
on her President’s face.

Come and see her. Come quickly. You will teach her to read, as you taught me, and you will be less
strict.

Your Blanche, who can’t sit still

::signature[Blanche]
