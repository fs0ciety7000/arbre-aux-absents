::stamp[VARENQUE & DESAULNES — BARRISTERS — 21 QUAI DES CHARTES, AUBRELIEU]

Aubrelieu, 25 September 1974

To the President of the Re-registration Commission,
care of Madam the Head of the Filiations Department,
Civil Re-registration Office.

**Subject:** file 74-0318 — objection to the testamentary petition of the late Miss Adélaïde
Varenque.

Mr President,

I have the honour to form an objection, in my own name and in the name of my father **Gaspard
Varenque**, deceased in 1971, whose memory I intend to defend, of my sister **Dr Hélène Marsal**, of
my wife **Mathilde** and of my aunt **Blanche Daubry**, to the execution of the third article of my
great-aunt’s will.

I am not attacking the bequest. Les Tilleuls will be divided as the law requires, and I shall be the
first to rejoice if cousins I have never heard of find something to their advantage there. I am
attacking the **petition**.

My great-aunt was ninety-four. She was, to her last day, possessed of an intelligence I envy her,
and of a taste for stagecraft that never left her: one does not draft a will in riddles, one does
not list “children” without naming them, when one simply wishes to be obeyed. One does so when one
wants others to go digging in one’s place.

Now, there is nothing to dig for, save what the Republic had the wisdom to judge in 1953. My father
was acquitted. He is dead. His children have borne the insinuations of the press for twenty years;
I shall not allow an administration to add its own under cover of genealogy. The Act of 14 May 1973
was passed to give their names back to the victims of the Regency, not to deliver up to **indecent
rummaging in the Regency papers** the honour of families who did nothing but survive.

Accordingly, I ask you:

1. to declare the petition inadmissible inasmuch as it names no one;
2. in the alternative, to forbid the release to the rapporteur of documents in the Varenque
   collection later than 1937, which belong to the private life of a family and not to civil
   registration;
3. to take formal note that I shall exercise, as heir, my right of family consultation of the said
   collection before any release.

I remain at the Commission’s disposal for the sitting of 15 November.

I have the honour to be, Mr President, yours faithfully,

::signature[Armand Varenque]

Maître Armand Varenque, barrister, chairman of the board of the Varenque Paper Mills
