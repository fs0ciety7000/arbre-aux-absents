*A sheet of ordinary Varenque paper, a small, neat hand in black ink. A greenish stain at the foot
of the page, like a burn.*

Aubrelieu, Sunday 2 October 1938

Aunt Adé,

You ask me why I stay in Aubrelieu on Sundays instead of coming home to Les Tilleuls for Mama’s leg
of lamb. Here is why: I have met someone.

His name is Orrin. He is a typesetter from Vellaak who can no longer earn his living from *Vel
Taalik* – you know why, everyone knows why, and nobody says it any more. Now he sets announcements
and menus in a printing works in the lower town, and he sings out of tune in Ostrian as he wedges
his lines. His fingers are black with ink; mine are burnt with acid. I think that is how we knew
each other.

He looked at one of my moulds the way nobody looks at them. He saw at once what I engrave and what
nobody sees. The others say: “pretty watermark”. He said: “Who taught you to write in water?”

At the workshop, nothing new. I am still the only woman engraver, and my forelock makes the
apprentices laugh: they say I leaned too close to the bleach. Since May, Gaspard has had us running
off a grey paper, and nobody will say what it is for. I know, and so do you.

Don’t say anything at Les Tilleuls. Not yet. I’ll bring him at Christmas, if you promise not to
make him recite his tables.

With love,

::signature[Odile]
