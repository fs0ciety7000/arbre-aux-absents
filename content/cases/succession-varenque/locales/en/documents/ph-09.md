## THE AUBRELIEU WASHERWOMEN’S CHARITY

**Relief, housing and schooling for laundresses and their children — founded 1909**

**Report for the year 1912, presented to the general meeting of 2 February 1913**

### Photograph (plate)

Full-page halftone. In front of the covered wash-house in the rue des Foulons, eight women pose.
In the centre, standing, a very tall lady with a long face and a dimple in her chin: ostrich-feather
hat, dark coat, a lorgnette hanging from her belt. Around her, seven women in aprons with their
sleeves rolled up, among them a young woman with curly black hair in a loose bun, on the lady’s
right, who smiles to show a gap between her front teeth; a small scar on her chin. A child of about
six clings to her skirt and looks elsewhere.

Caption: *Mrs Augustin Varenque, president, and our protégées.*

### The President’s address

Ladies and Gentlemen,

Our Charity is entering its fourth year. It has not changed its mind: a woman who washes the linen
of a whole town must be able to wash her own children’s as well, in hot water, under a roof. This
is not philanthropy; it is plumbing.

This year, thanks to you, the wash-house in the rue des Foulons has been roofed, heated and lit;
forty-two of our protégées have received housing relief; nineteen children have been placed in
school and shod for the winter. We have opened an interest-free loan fund, and two loans in three
have already been repaid, which is more than any bank of my acquaintance can say.

I thank our benefactors, and in particular those who wished to remain anonymous. They will know
who they are; so do we.

::signature[The President, Mrs Augustin Varenque]
