**THE ORVELLE GAZETTE** — Moulinvert — Friday 15 March 1974 — 0.40 Fn

## DEATHS

### Miss Adélaïde Varenque, the schoolmistress of Moulinvert

*“Aunt Adé” passed away on Tuesday at Les Tilleuls, aged ninety-four. Three generations of Moulinvert girls owe it to her that they can read.*

**Miss Adélaïde Varenque** died on Tuesday 12 March, early in the morning, in her bedroom at Les Tilleuls, the house where she was born on 1 December 1879 and which, in all her life, she had left only for short journeys. She was ninety-four. She was the **last of the children of Anselme** Varenque, founder of the Paper Mills, and of Clémence Varenque, the botanist; she had seen both her brothers die, and many of the family’s children. She used to say she was staying on “to keep the accounts”.

Having started at twenty as **schoolmistress** at the girls’ school of Moulinvert, she was appointed its **headmistress** in 1905 and remained so until she retired in 1944: **forty-five years** of school, without a day’s illness, her former colleagues declare, and without one unjust punishment, her former pupils declare. The title of headmistress never suited her; no one in Moulinvert ever called her anything but “the schoolmistress”, or, for three generations of little girls and for their children, **Aunt Adé**.

She taught handwriting with the pen, her mother’s botany, the history of the country without skipping its bad pages and, in the years when it was forbidden, a few words of Ostrian, “because a language no one learns any more is a family no one visits any more”. She would never say how, under the Regency, the girls’ school had managed to keep its enrolment registers intact when those of so many other schools were being “brought up to date”. Her pupils remember it for her.

In December 1969 the *Gazette* had celebrated her ninetieth birthday with her. She received us in the drawing room at Les Tilleuls, upright in her armchair, her fob watch pinned to her breast, and as she saw us out she told us: “Write that I am old, but don’t write that I am wise. I still have things to do.”

The funeral will be held on Saturday at ten o’clock in the church at Moulinvert. The burial will take place in the family vault. In accordance with the wishes of the deceased, mourners are asked not to bring flowers, but to leave at the girls’ school “a book you have loved”.

### Miss Varenque’s dispositions

It is said in Moulinvert that Miss Varenque left a will and that her family papers, which are many, are to go to the National Archives. The family notary declines all comment before the opening, set for next week.
