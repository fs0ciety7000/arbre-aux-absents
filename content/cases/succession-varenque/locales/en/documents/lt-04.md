**WASHERWOMEN’S CHARITY**
Founded in 1909 — Relief, firewood, soap and the doctor’s visit for the laundresses of Aubrelieu
Office: 3 rue du Lavoir — **The President**

Aubrelieu, 11 March 1913

My dear Adélaïde,

I am writing to you what I shall tell no one else, and I ask you to keep it to yourself in the same
way.

Since last autumn, I have had a laundress from the rue des Tanneurs, Pauline Sauge, and her boy,
who is six, enrolled in our Washerwomen’s Charity. She has the firewood, the soap and the doctor’s
visit, like the others, and nothing more: I will not have her singled out. She appears in our annual
report between a widow and a woman whose husband drinks, and that is just as it should be.

She does not know that I know. I shall not tell you whom the child resembles; you know it better
than I do, and you knew it before I did.

Augustin has no idea what I do with my Charity, as he has no idea of a great many things he has
decided not to know. I shall not enlighten him. In this family there are men who make laws and
women who do what has to be done. We know how to keep silent better than they do; it is the only
superiority they leave us, so let us make use of it.

Burn this if you wish. I would not, in your place.

Your sister,

::signature[Henriette]
