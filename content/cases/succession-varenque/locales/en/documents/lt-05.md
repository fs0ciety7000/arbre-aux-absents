*A grey military letter-card, written in indelible pencil. One line has been blacked out by the
postal censor.*

::stamp[ARMY POSTAL CENSORSHIP — PASSES SECTOR — SEEN]

With the army, 20 October 1917

Adé,

I had my leave at the start of the month. I did what I told you I would: I went by way of
Saint-Ormel, to Maître Brunesseau, the notary on the place de la Cathédrale, and I signed the deed
by which I acknowledge Victor. In due and proper form, with two clerks for witnesses trying not to
yawn, and my statement written in my own hand, without a single mistake. It is to be put forward if
anything happens to me.

Nothing will happen to me, naturally. Here we paint rocks on canvas so that the shells go for the
wrong rock, and the shells, so far, seem to read the instructions. We have been at the
::redacted[17] for three weeks, and it is cold enough to split the brushes.

My will of last year makes Augustin my executor. I have not had time to change it, and the notary
says the deed of acknowledgement is enough, that it stands on its own. If anyone ever tells you that
the deed does not exist, go to Saint-Ormel and ask to see it. Notaries forget nothing.

Don’t tell Victor anything. He is eleven; he thinks his father is at sea. I would rather be at sea
than in a ditch.

With love. Keep me a place at the Christmas table, even an empty one.

::signature[Lucien — army painter, camouflage section]

::hand[In Adélaïde’s hand, on the back: Received 2 November 1917. He was at the Col de Corvenne. A.]
