**FERN** — What burns grows back — No. 37 — June 1944 — Not for sale. Read it, copy it, pass it on, burn it.

## AUGUSTIN V., TWENTY, STUDENT, SHOT

They shot him on Saturday 17 June, at first light, in the ditch of the Citadel. The same day, before noon, their notice was pasted on the walls we had covered three nights earlier. They pasted their paper over ours. It is all they know how to do.

His name was **Augustin V.** We do not give his full name: they have it, they have printed it, but he has a family, and we know what the Regency does to families. He was **twenty**. He was a **student** of letters at the University of Aubrelieu, in his second year. He read the Ostrian poets in the original, which is forbidden, and the Velmarchois poets without skipping a line, which is rare.

For two years he carried our parcels. He went through the checkpoints with a satchel of lecture notes, grammar exercises and, under the notes, what allowed others to live. He knew the way through the forest better than we did. He never asked us what he was carrying. We told him, once, who it was for. He smiled. He said: “So she’s alive?” and we did not answer, and he smiled all the more.

### The Night of the Posters

On the night of 14 to 15 June, at the call of *Fern*, forty teams covered the walls of Aubrelieu with the poster you have seen: an open register, a fern growing between the pages, and these words: **THEY WILL NOT RUB US OUT.** That was the **Night of the Posters**. For three nights the Guard of Order washed the walls. For three nights we covered them again.

Augustin was taken on the second night, in the rue des Tanneurs, a paste pot in his hand. He gave nobody away. We know it because nobody was taken after him.

### What we ask

Do not weep over the walls. Copy this sheet. Teach this name to your children, half of it, as we do: one day we shall write it in full, in an honest register, on a paper that does not lie.
