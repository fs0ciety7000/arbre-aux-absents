::stamp[CERTIFIED TRUE COPY — BRUNESSAU NOTARY’S OFFICE, SAINT-ORMEL]

Moulinvert, at Les Tilleuls, the second of January nineteen hundred and seventy-four.

This is my will. I write it alone, in my own hand, in full possession of my faculties, which are
still sound, whatever my great-nephew may say.

I am Adélaïde Varenque, born at Les Tilleuls on the first of December 1879, daughter of my father
**Anselme** and my mother **Clémence**, the last living of their three children. I was schoolmistress
at the girls’ school of Moulinvert for forty-five years. I taught half this town to read; the other
half, I taught to keep quiet. It is for that I wish to apologise.

**First.** I give to the National Archives all the family papers to be found at Les Tilleuls:
letters, photographs, registers, notebooks, my mother’s herbarium and everything contained in the
bureau in the drawing room and the trunk in the attic. I ask that they be **entrusted to the
conservation workshop**, and to no one else before it. They are old, they are fragile, and some of
them have never been read by those to whom they were addressed.

**Second.** I bequeath my house, Les Tilleuls, and the shares in the Paper Mills that I hold from
my father **to all the living descendants of my father Anselme and my uncle Firmin**, in equal
parts, without distinction of branch, name, language or birth, as the Civil Re-registration Office
shall have established them.

I insist on that last word. I do not want them to make do with those who turn up at the funeral.

**Third.** Under the Act of 14 May 1973, I ask that those we made [[absent|absents]] be sought out
and re-registered. I know that the law asks for two documents for each of them. I leave the
documents. I do not leave my opinion: I was taught that it is worth nothing before a court, and I am
past the age of arguing with courts.

So let them search, in this order or in another:

- my mother **Clémence**, who drew our fern;
- my brothers **Augustin** and **Lucien**, who did not speak to each other for twelve years and died
  unreconciled;
- Augustin’s children, my nephew and niece **Gaspard** and **Blanche**, and my niece **Odile**,
  ~~who is dead~~ who was taken from me;
- our cousins from the **Tennok** lighthouse, whom the family always received in the kitchen rather
  than the drawing room, which was a disgrace and a mistake, for it is in the kitchen that people
  talk;
- **Lucien’s child**, whom my brother Augustin chose to ignore;
- **Odile’s child**, born where no one is born.

I do not write their names. It is not affectation. If I write them, I shall be believed, or I shall
not be believed, and in either case nothing will be checked. I would rather it were checked.

**Fourth.** I beg my great-nephew Armand not to form an objection to this will. I know that he will.
I forgive him in advance, on condition that he [[reads|lise]] the file to the end.

I appoint as my executor Maître Brunesseau, notary at Saint-Ormel, whose grandfather once kept, in
his deed archive, a paper he had been asked to forget. Notaries forget nothing; that is why I am
fond of them.

Done in a single original, at Les Tilleuls, by the fireside, with my mother’s watch pinned to my
collar.

::signature[Adélaïde Varenque]
