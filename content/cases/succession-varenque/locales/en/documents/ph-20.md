## VEL TAALIK — the Messenger of the Sea

**Second series — No. 1 — Vellaak, May 1953 — 0.15 Fn — Banned from 1938 to 1952. Back.**

### Selda Varenque returns from exile

She stepped off the Port-Sénane launch with a suitcase, a typewriter and fifteen years of post to
catch up on. Selda Varenque, editor of *Vel Taalik* until it was banned in February 1938, struck off
the same week and exiled to Brenzac, on the far side of the pass, set foot once more on the quay at
Vellaak last Thursday, in a fine rain that nobody noticed.

“I spent fifteen years writing articles that nobody published,” she told us. “I kept them. You are
going to read them, whether you like it or not.”

She was met at the end of the jetty by her brother, Firmin Varenque, skipper of the *Maaren*, who
kept the boat in for the day, something Vellaak had not seen since the storm of 1924, and by his
son, Tavik, keeper of the light at Tennok, who had come on foot across the moor and set off back the
same evening, “because the light doesn’t light itself”.

*Vel Taalik* reappears today under her direction, in the two rooms above the drying shed on the
jetée Est, with the same treadle press, found at the house of a fisherman who had hidden it under
his nets for fifteen years. The Ostrian type case was lost. The *aa* and *ee* in this number were
cut by hand, one by one.

### Photograph

On the quay, in front of the black hull of a fishing boat. In the centre, a woman of about fifty
with grey hair cropped very short, a cigarillo between her lips, thin spectacles hanging on a chain
over her coat. On her left, a man of about fifty, short grey beard, a gold ring in his left ear, a
zigzag jersey, a seabird with spread wings tattooed on his right forearm; he is carrying the
suitcase. On her right, a very tall young man in his twenties, red-haired, beardless, a gold ring in
his left ear, who does not know what to do with his hands.
