*Two sheets in a fine, upright hand, without a single crossing-out. A tracing folded in four is
pinned to the second: a fern frond drawn in pen, dimensioned to the millimetre.*

Les Tilleuls, 22 April 1888

My dear Anselme,

You asked me for a drawing for your new watermark, “something that shows against the light and
doesn’t look like a grocer’s”. Here it is. It is the royal fern from sheet 27 of my herbarium,
picked on the bank of the Clairette in the spring of 1871, the finest I have. I have simplified the
pinnae so that your mould-cutter won’t break his fingers on them, and I have measured it: it fits
in the corner of a quarto sheet without touching the laid lines.

My father – Mr Aubertot to you, always, after eighteen years of marriage – saw it this morning and
said it was too beautiful for paper. I told him that nothing is too beautiful for paper: it is
where we write the things that last.

The Aubrelieu Botanical Society has received my note on the ferns of the Clairette and is asking me
for a book, with plates. I said yes, on condition that the Paper Mills make my paper. I am counting
on you not to charge me what you charge strangers.

All is well here. Augustin toils at his Latin, sighing like a widower. Lucien has painted the cat
blue – the cat, not a portrait of the cat. Our little Adé, who has my forelock, like Augustin, asked
me whether one could make a watermark of a person, “so you could see them against the light when
they aren’t there any more”. I didn’t know what to answer. You answer her.

Don’t quarrel with the rag merchants of Hautcorvin: the cut through your eyebrow is reputation
enough.

::signature[Your Clémence]
