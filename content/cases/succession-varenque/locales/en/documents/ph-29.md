## VEL TAALIK — the Messenger of the Sea — second series, No. 1012 — October 1972 — 0.30 Fn

### Tavik Varenque, the last keeper of Tennok

Since 1 June, the Tennok light has lit itself. A photo-cell switches it on at dusk, a clock switches
it off at dawn, and a cable links it to the Lights and Beacons station at Port-Sénane, which now
knows before anyone else when a lamp has blown. Automation has cost, the administration tells us,
the price of eleven years of a keeper’s pay. The administration has done its sums.

The keeper, for his part, has not left.

Tavik Varenque had tended the Tennok light since 1950; he was eighteen. He is forty now. He still
lives in the house at the foot of the tower, which Lights and Beacons lets him have free of charge,
“for surveillance”, which gives them a keeper who costs nothing. Every evening he still climbs up,
out of habit, to watch the lamp come on without him. “Somebody has to check that it does,” he says.

He speaks little, slowly, in Ostrian first. He draws the birds of the cliff in notebooks that he
shows to no one, except, that day, to our photographer, who was not allowed to photograph them. He
wears a zigzag jersey that belonged to his grandfather and that has been darned more often than it
was ever knitted.

We asked him whether he had regrets. He thought for a long time. “The light never needed me. It’s
the boats. The boats need someone to watch.”

On the gallery of the lighthouse, leaning on the rail, a very tall man of about forty, full red
