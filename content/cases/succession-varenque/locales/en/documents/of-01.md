::stamp[REPUBLIC OF VELMARCHE — CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILIATIONS DEPARTMENT]

Aubrelieu, 7 October 1974

**Subject:** file No. 74-0318 — Estate of Adélaïde Varenque, testamentary petition for
re-registration.

**To:** probationary rapporteur, office 12, third floor.

**Enclosures:** 2 to 6 (see schedule at the end of this note).

Dear Rapporteur,

I am entrusting you with the file named above. It is your first; it will not be the simplest. I am
entrusting it to you all the same, because the longer-serving members of the department have all,
in one way or another, heard of this family already, and I need a pair of eyes that has heard
nothing.

## 1. The petition

Miss Adélaïde Varenque, retired schoolmistress, died at Moulinvert on 12 March last, in her house
known as Les Tilleuls. By holograph will of 2 January 1974 (item 2), she gave the Varenque
collection to the National Archives, bequeathed her property “to all the living descendants” of her
father and her uncle, and petitioned the State, under the Act of 14 May 1973, for the
re-registration of “those we made absent”.

The two stocks of descent are:

- **Anselme Varenque**, papermaker, founder of the Varenque Paper Mills at Moulinvert (1868), father of the testatrix;
- **Firmin Varenque**, his younger brother.

Both are sons of Jacques Varenque, miller on the Clairette, whose descendants are not in question
beyond these two branches.

## 2. What the Commission expects of you

You will draw up the **table of descent** of Anselme Varenque and Firmin Varenque. For each person,
the regulation form (form ORC-3) requires three headings:

1. **identity**: usual given name and surname at birth (names in customary use that appear in the certificates are admitted);
2. **portrait identification**: you will match the person to a face attested by a photograph or a
   reproduction placed on the file;
3. **status**: the condition or occupation under which the person is known.

I would remind you of Article 6 of the Act: *no identity is admitted unless two concordant documents
establish it.* The Commission does not examine the cards one by one; it stamps a coherent whole or
sends it back. An incomplete branch does not pass. Nor does a false one.

I would also remind you that portrait identification is not a formality. The Regency falsified a
great many certificates; it was less good at falsifying faces.

Miss Adélaïde Varenque herself already appears in the table (items 2 and 3). For her father, the
name and status are settled; you lack a face. For Maître Armand Varenque, who has formed an
objection (item 4), the situation is the same.

## 3. Your means

- The **LX-2 Reader** in the reading room of the National Archives, which queries the **Central
  Indexed Collection**. The operating instructions are enclosed (item 5). Read them: the Reader does
  not forgive approximation, and nor does the computing department.
- Documents are brought to your office on reference, in original or on microfiche.
- The wall map in office 12 gives you access to the collections arranged by place.
- **Restricted collections**: the archives of the Census and Removals Bureau, the Kessaar penal
  colony and the Wards’ Charity are released only on an exact request by name (Act of 14 May 1973,
  Art. 9). The Reader will return them only if you give it a given name and a surname, exactly. It
  will not help you find them.

## 4. Deadline

The sitting of the Re-registration Commission is set for **15 November 1974**. The Civil
Re-registration Office was created to give the absent back their names; it was not created to give
them back late.

I remain at your disposal, door 4, mornings.

::signature[R. Ferrandot]

Rosine Ferrandot, head of the Filiations Department
