*Two squared sheets torn from a school exercise book, written in violet ink, steeply slanted. A
round cigarillo burn at the foot of the second sheet.*

Vellaak, 20 May 1953

Dear Adélaïde,

Fifteen years. I came back from Brenzac over the pass, in a coach that smelt of thyme, diesel and
the fear of those who did not quite believe it yet. At the border, the customs officer handed back
my papers saying “welcome home, madam”. I did not know one could weep over a sentence from a customs
officer.

Our mother Tenna died without seeing me again, in December ’50, the same month as your
sister-in-law Henriette. I learnt it in Brenzac from a card that had taken six weeks. I went to the
grave yesterday; she has a headscarf of stone, it is her all right.

My brother Firmin, the Gull, came to meet me at the quay with his son. He has aged all at once, or
else I have. He has never forgiven himself for that night in November ’24. He told me so again last
night, with the name of the boat he only says after the third glass: the *Albatros*. I think he
spends his life bringing people back to land to give one back to the sea, and the sum will never
come out right.

Young Tavik has been keeper in his turn, of the Tennok light, for three years now. Twenty years old,
two metres tall, not three words a day: a real keeper. He draws the birds in the light’s register,
between the wind readings. Nobody has told him it isn’t allowed; nobody dares.

*Vel Taalik* came out again on Saturday, two days after I got back, because our friends had got
everything ready without me: four pages, two languages, a single typewriter, bought in Brenzac,
which already has opinions about certain letters.

With love. You are seventy-three and still have no vices: it is suspicious.

::signature[Selda]
