**THE ORVELLE GAZETTE** — Moulinvert — Published Wednesdays and Saturdays — Wednesday 17 May 1905 — 5 sous

## ELECTIONS TO THE CHAMBER

### Mr Augustin Varenque elected Deputy for the Orvelle

*The son of the Moulinvert papermaker wins by four hundred and thirty-two votes over the sitting member. Torchlight procession and bonfire on the square before the Town House.*

The electors of the Orvelle constituency delivered their verdict on Sunday, and it admits no appeal, if no surprise either: **Mr Augustin Varenque**, the Liberal Union candidate, is **elected Deputy** with 6,412 votes against 5,980 for Mr Théodule Grangier, the outgoing Radical member, who had sat in the Chamber for twelve years. There were 12,611 voters, 219 blank or spoilt papers having been set aside by the counting office.

The result was proclaimed at eleven o’clock at night from the steps of the Town House of Moulinvert. A considerable crowd, in which one noticed many workmen from the Paper Mills in their Sunday best, at once struck up the tune of *La Clairette*, and a torchlight procession, which no one had officially organised but which everyone seemed to expect, escorted the new member as far as the gates of Les Tilleuls, where Mr Anselme Varenque, his father, had beer served to anyone who wanted it. A great many did.

### A young industrialist in the Chamber

Mr Augustin Varenque is thirty-three. The elder son of Mr Anselme Varenque, founder of the Varenque Paper Mills, he read law at Aubrelieu before joining his father’s house, whose government custom he has built up. He is married to Miss Henriette Sorbelle, daughter of the well-known Aubrelieu banker. He is said to be an orator; he proved it during the campaign, at forty-two public meetings, one of which, at Hautcorvin, ended in a battle of chairs that left everyone delighted.

His opponents reproached him with his youth, his fortune and his posters, which, it is true, covered even the stable doors. His friends reply that the constituency needs a young industrialist who knows what a ton of rags costs, and a member who will speak in the Chamber of the Clairette and its mills. The outgoing member, a good loser, told our correspondent yesterday: “Mr Varenque has won; now he must deserve it.”

### What the new member said

In a short speech from the steps, Mr Varenque thanked “the electors of every opinion who were willing to trust a man of paper, that is to say a man who knows what he is signing”. He promised to press in the Chamber for a bridge over the Clairette below Moulinvert, for the protection of Velmarchois paper against foreign competition, and for electric light in the market towns of the valley. He ended by saluting “my father, who taught me never to promise anything I cannot deliver”.

Among those present were Mrs Augustin Varenque and Miss Adélaïde Varenque, the new member’s sister, appointed this spring headmistress of our girls’ school, whom her former pupils were already calling by her Christian name in the middle of the crowd. Mr Lucien Varenque, the second son, a painter in Aubrelieu, had been unable to make the journey.
