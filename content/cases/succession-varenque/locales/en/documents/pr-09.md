**THE HERALD** — Organ of Velmarchois Order — Tuesday 21 October 1941 — 40 sous

## “FERN” NETWORK SMASHED AT THE MOULIN DE LA SORBE

### A forged-papers factory discovered in the forest, two leagues from the capital

*Thanks to the vigilance of Commissioner Daubry, the Guard of Order on Sunday put an end to the activities of a den which for a year had been supplying false identities to the enemies of the Nation. Two arrests.*

They thought themselves safe among the beeches. They thought themselves cleverer than Order. They were wrong on both counts.

On Sunday 19 October, at dawn, a detachment of the Guard of Order surrounded the **Moulin de la Sorbe**, that old paper mill abandoned since the last century deep in the Sorbe forest, between Aubrelieu and Moulinvert, where Sunday walkers hardly go any more except to pick mushrooms. There, beneath the rubble of the vat room, was found a complete forgers’ workshop: a hand press, cases of type, inks, counterfeit stamps of several Town Houses, paper moulds and several hundred identity books, blank or already filled in with the names of persons struck off.

The den called itself, with a poetry our readers will appreciate, the **“Fern” network**. Its leaflets, which some of you may have found slipped under your door, ended with this motto: “What burns grows back.” We shall see.

### Two arrests

Two individuals were arrested on the spot, caught in the act of printing: **the engraver Odile V.**, thirty-three, employed until that day at a paper mill in the valley, and **the Ostrian printer K.**, thirty-six, of no fixed abode in Aubrelieu since the banning of the separatist sheet on which he practised his talents. The woman, who engraved the moulds used to imitate the watermarks of official papers, still bore on her hands the marks of the acids of her trade. The man tried to throw a bundle of documents into the vat; it was fished out.

The two accused were taken to the Citadel, where the Census and Removals Bureau is preparing their file. They are expected to appear on the next removal list of the *Bulletin of Order*.

### Vigilance pays

This exemplary operation is owed, on the admission of the commander of the Guard of Order himself, to **the vigilance of Commissioner Daubry**, Commissioner of Registers for the province, who had for several weeks noticed certain anomalies in the books presented at checks and who was able, through a personal piece of information whose source he declined to reveal, to trace them back to the mill. Yesterday the Commissioner received the congratulations of the Regent.

The paper seized at the mill came, according to the first findings, from thefts committed to the detriment of a great house of Moulinvert, whose management has given the inquiry its full assistance and whose loyalty is not in question.
