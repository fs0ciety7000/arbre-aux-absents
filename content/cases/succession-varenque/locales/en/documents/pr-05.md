**THE AUBRELIEU COURIER** — Daily — Monday 24 November 1924 — 12 sous

## STORM ON THE MER GRISE

### Tennok lifeboat coxswain lost saving three unknown castaways

TENNOK, 23 November (by telegram). — The storm which swept the whole north coast on Saturday, and whose effects our readers could measure even in the streets of Aubrelieu, has claimed at Tennok a victim for whom all Ostrie is mourning tonight.

At about half past one in the morning, in the night of the 21st to the 22nd, the Tennok light signalled a vessel in distress on the **Dents de Tennok**, that string of reefs the old people call, in Ostrian, *the teeth of the stone*. In a sea the men of the cape describe as “the worst in forty years”, the **Tennok lifeboat** put out at two o’clock under the command of her coxswain, **Mr Aldrik Varenque**, fifty, who had held her helm for eighteen years.

### Three men brought back

The lifeboat managed to come alongside the vessel, a small motor cutter named, it is believed, the *Albatros*, which carried neither registration nor flag. Three men were snatched from the wreck and hauled aboard. It was while going forward to throw a last line that Mr Varenque was swept away by a wave. His crew searched for him until dawn. The sea has not given him back.

The three survivors, who were cared for at the keeper’s house, refused to give their names to the constables from Vellaak. Down at the harbour they say the cutter came from Thalberie and that her cargo had never set eyes on a customs officer. The inquiry by Lights and Beacons will tell. For the moment, here, people speak only of the man who was lost.

### A family of the sea

Mr Aldrik Varenque was the son of the late **Captain Firmin Varenque**, of the Orvelle Packet Company, and of **Mrs Maaren Varenque**, who was herself, a rare thing, **a woman keeper of the light at Tennok** for nearly thirty years. He leaves a widow, **Mrs Tenna Orrvik**, **postmistress** of Tennok, well known to everyone who has ever sent a card from the cape, and two children already grown.

He was, writes the present keeper, Mr Jorre Haask, “a man who said little and was never wrong about the weather”. Yet he was wrong on Saturday, or he pretended to be.

The funeral, without a body, will take place on Thursday at Tennok according to the Ostrian rite: a lantern will be set upon the water.
