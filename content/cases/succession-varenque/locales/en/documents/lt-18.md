*Printed letterhead, a letter typed on an electric typewriter without a single typing error; the
signature is in felt-tip.*

**VELMARCHE CALCULATING COMPANY**
Documentary Systems Division — Aubrelieu

Aubrelieu, 4 December 1973

Dear Madam,

Your letter was passed on to me by the National Archives, who did not know whom to give it to: it
was addressed “to the woman engineer on the Reader”. That is me. There is no other.

You ask me three questions; I shall answer them in order.

One. Yes, the LX-2 Reader finds everything the indexers have recorded. No, it finds nothing else.
It does not guess, it does not correct, it does not understand circumlocutions. If you ask it for a
person, ask for that person by given name and surname, exactly. That is its virtue and its fault; I
have not managed to separate the two.

Two. No, I am not related to the Sauges of Hautcorvin. I am related to no one, as far as I know: my
father, Victor Sauge, the aviator, died in 1966; my mother in 1971. I know nothing of my paternal
grandfather. My father used to say he was born of a washerwoman and a poster. He said it laughing.
He laughed at everything that hurt him; it is a habit I have kept.

Three. Why should this interest me? I do not know. I am a woman engineer; I do not like incomplete
records. If you know something, write it to me. I promise not to index it.

Yours faithfully,

::signature[Claude Sauge, woman engineer at the CVC]
