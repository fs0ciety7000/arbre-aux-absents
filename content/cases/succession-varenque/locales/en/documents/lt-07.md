**AÉRO-VELMARCHE** — Air Mail
Plaine-aux-Saules Aerodrome, Aubrelieu

Aubrelieu, 12 August 1931

Dear Madam,

Your letter reached me safely. People have written me a great many since the July record flight:
young ladies, postcard sellers, a gentleman who wanted to know whether you can see God above the Mer
Grise (you cannot). Yours is the only one I have read twice.

You say you knew my father. My mother never speaks of him. When I was small, she said he was at
sea; when I was grown up, she stopped saying anything at all. What I tell the newspapers, you have
read: you have to answer something when someone pushes a microphone at you, and a captain lost at
sea makes a fine sentence.

You say he painted.

I do not know what to do with that sentence. I took it up with me in the aeroplane on Tuesday, on
the Hautcorvin route. I bring it back to you, with a question: is he alive?

If you wish to answer me, write to me at the aerodrome. My mother does not read my post, but she
knows people’s handwriting, and yours is a fine one.

With my respect,

::signature[Victor Sauge, pilot and aviator]
