**THE AUBRELIEU COURIER** — Daily — Sunday 19 July 1931 — 15 sous

## VICTOR SAUGE’S FLIGHT

### Aubrelieu–Kessaar–Aubrelieu: the Mer Grise crossed by air mail

*Leaving the Plaine-aux-Saules at dawn yesterday, the Aéro-Velmarche aviator dropped the mail over the island of Kessaar and returned to base in six hours and twelve minutes. A first for Velmarche.*

It is done. What only this spring was still taken for a café wager, an air-mail link between the capital and the islands of the Mer Grise, the aviator **Victor Sauge** accomplished yesterday aboard *L’Hirondelle*, mail biplane No. 4 of the **Aéro-Velmarche** company, with no other mishap than a temperamental magneto and a seagull which, he says, “was absolutely determined to make the trip with me”.

Taking off at five o’clock from the Plaine-aux-Saules aerodrome, the machine followed the Orvelle up to Grisenoue, flew over the Landes d’Ostrie at fifteen hundred metres, then struck out over the Mer Grise in a fog which, on the pilot’s testimony, “began at the waterline and stopped nowhere”. It reached **Kessaar** at twenty to eight. The island, which no aeroplane had ever flown over, offers no landing ground: Victor Sauge therefore dropped, in three passes over the little lighthouse, a weighted mailbag fitted with a red streamer, which the keeper and his wife retrieved on the rock. The bag held fourteen hundred letters, among them one from the Minister of Posts, and a sugarloaf for the keeper’s children.

The return, with the wind behind, was quicker. *L’Hirondelle* landed at the Plaine-aux-Saules at twelve minutes past eleven, before two thousand people who had heard the news on the wireless and who carried the aviator shoulder-high to the hangar, where he asked, in this order, for a coffee, a cigarette and news of the seagull.

### Who is Victor Sauge?

He is twenty-five, with a matinée idol’s dimples and an eye which, by his comrades’ own admission, “looks a little to one side, which doesn’t stop him seeing better than we do”. Born in Aubrelieu, he learnt to fly at twenty, saving out of his mechanic’s wages, and he has been a mail pilot for Aéro-Velmarche since the company was founded in 1928. He already has to his credit four hundred Aubrelieu–Port-Sénane runs and the rescue, last winter, of a mailbag that had fallen into the Clairette.

When we asked him where this taste for the open sea came from, he answered, laughing: “From my father. A captain from Port-Sénane, lost at sea when I was very small. I never knew him; I was told he looked at the horizon the way I do, crooked.” Then he added, more quietly: “My mother doesn’t like it talked about. Just put that she’s proud.”

She may well be.

### And now?

The management of Aéro-Velmarche has made no secret of its intention to open, from next spring, a regular line to Vellaak and the lighthouses of the north coast. The Minister of Posts, who had come to the field to meet the aviator, presented Victor Sauge with the Air Mail medal and begged him, next time, “to take a little less sugar and a few more letters”.
