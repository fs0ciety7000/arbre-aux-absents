## THE ORVELLE GAZETTE — 15 September 1956 — Social Notes

### A wedding at the Paper Mills

On Saturday, at the Town House of Moulinvert and then in the church of Notre-Dame-de-la-Clairette,
was celebrated the wedding of **Dr Hélène Varenque**, daughter of the Director of the Paper Mills,
and **Mr Paul Marsal**, journalist on the *Courier*, in Aubrelieu.

The bride, whom many of our lady readers know from having entrusted their children to her care, had
insisted on a simple ceremony: a white crêpe gown without a train, a short veil, and her spectacles,
which she refused to take off “so as to see who I am saying yes to”. The groom, a son of
Saint-Ormel, made his name with his reports on the April Days, from which, it is said, he keeps the
nose he broke on a barricade in the Tanneurs quarter. He assured us it was a door.

The reception, at Les Tilleuls, brought together some hundred guests. The eldest, who was
schoolmistress to the bride and to half the company, spoke a few words that we shall not report, at
her request, but which made people laugh and weep.

Our best wishes for their happiness to the young couple, who will settle in Port-Sénane.

### Photograph

On the steps of the Town House. A young woman of about twenty-five, short curly hair, tortoiseshell
spectacles, a cleft chin, a simple white dress and short veil, holds the arm of a man barely thirty,
curly-haired, in a dark suit, whose nose has been broken and badly set. They are both laughing at
something happening outside the frame.
