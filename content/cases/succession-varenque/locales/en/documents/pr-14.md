**THE ORVELLE GAZETTE** — Moulinvert — Monday 17 July 1961 — 0.25 Fn

## THE SEASON AT BAINS-DE-MÉRANDE

### Blanche Aubertine’s farewell at the Thermes de Mérande

*A full house, bathers in tears, and a soprano who chose to end on a fishermen’s song.*

She had announced it in the spring, and no one wanted to believe it: on Saturday evening, in the great hall of the **Thermes de Mérande**, **Blanche Aubertine** gave her last concert. At sixty, after a career of thirty-seven years, twenty-eight of them at the Aubrelieu Opera, the **singer** whom two generations of Velmarchois applauded in *The Lantern of Ostrie* leaves the stage as she came on to it: without fuss, in a superb gown and with a voice one would have sworn was untouched. The gown, of pearl-grey faille embroidered with little lanterns, is the work of Mrs Ninon Sauge, née Brial, head costume maker at the Aubrelieu Opera, who dressed her for twenty years and had made the journey to put in the last pin herself.

The programme, chosen by the artist herself, ranged from court airs of the old duchy to the songs of Mr Aurèle Vasquin, by way of three folk songs of the north coast, sung in Ostrian, which would have been unthinkable in that hall ten years ago. The great lantern aria, from the third act of the work that made her name, was encored, then encored again. Mrs Aubertine refused to sing it a fourth time: “I have sung it enough for the drowned,” she told the audience. “Tonight I am keeping it for myself.”

### A family audience

Bains-de-Mérande, where the artist has lived in retirement since 1952 and where she may be met every morning at the pump room, had been determined to make the evening a celebration. The mayor sat in the front row, surrounded by the town council and the management of the Thermes; the pupils of the girls’ high school, where the singer has given free singing lessons for nine years, formed a guard of honour at the entrance.

Also noticed, in the second row, having come from Moulinvert especially, was **Miss Adélaïde Varenque**, eighty-one, the former headmistress of our girls’ school and the artist’s aunt, who, it is said, had not missed a single one of her first nights. She listened to the whole concert without moving, her hand on her fob watch, and we are assured that in the interval she did not take her eyes off the piano.

For **her daughter Lise was turning the pages**. The young girl, nineteen, who intends, we are told, to devote herself to the restoration of old documents, performed her task with a gravity that moved the house. At the final bow the singer brought her forward to the edge of the stage to share the flowers. One could see that mother and daughter were not much alike: the one is dark, the other has pretty fair curls, usually held back by a ribbon; the ribbon slipped during the bow, and above her brow appeared a forelock of pure white, a strange thing at nineteen. One could also see that they laughed in the same way.

### And afterwards?

“Afterwards?” Mrs Aubertine said to our chronicler. “At last I shall be able to listen to other people.” She added that she would go on with her lessons at the high school, “as long as the girls will have me”.

The season at the Thermes continues until 30 September. Next concert: Saturday 22 July, the Hellier Quartet, in Mr Vasquin’s quartets.
