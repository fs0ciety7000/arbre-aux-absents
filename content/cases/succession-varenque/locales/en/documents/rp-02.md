## REPORT TO THE DIRECTOR-GENERAL OF THE BRR

### Subject: “Operation Fern” — dismantling of the forged-papers workshop at the moulin de la Sorbe

Aubrelieu, 20 October 1941.

The Commissioner of Registers for the province of the Orvelle, **Marcel Daubry**, has the honour to report the following facts.

**1. Origin of the case.** Since February, checks in the province have intercepted forty-one counterfeit identity booklets of very fine workmanship, all bearing as a watermark a fern copied from the one on official papers, but of a finer design. Examination against the light has established that these watermarks could only be the work of a trained mould-maker familiar with the methods of the Varenque Paper Mills.

**2. Intelligence.** The decisive intelligence was obtained by the undersigned **within his own family by marriage**. On several occasions, at family gatherings, the undersigned observed recent **acid burns** on the right hand of **the woman O. V.**, watermark engraver at the Varenque Paper Mills and sister of his wife, whose workshop has used no acid since the spring. He further noticed her repeated absences from the family home on Sundays. Discreet shadowing, entrusted to two plain-clothes officers from 28 September to 12 October, made it possible to follow her to the moulin de la Sorbe, where she met an Ostrian, the man K., formerly typesetter on the banned sheet *Vel Taalik*.

**3. Operation.** On Sunday 19 October, at 5.30, the Guard of Order surrounded the mill at the requisition of the undersigned. Arrested in the act: **VARENQUE Odile**, engraver, and **KESSVEL Orrin**, typesetter, Ostrian. Seized: a hand press, two type cases, three engraved paper moulds, one hundred and ninety blank booklets, fourteen completed booklets, and a distribution register partly in cipher. The other members of the workshop have not been identified to date; the register is under study.

**4. The family’s position.** The director of the Paper Mills, Mr G. Varenque, brother of the accused woman, was not informed of the operation before it was carried out. Nothing shows that he had any knowledge of it. It is not desirable that he should learn the source of the intelligence. Mrs Daubry, wife of the undersigned, knows nothing of it and must know nothing.

**5. Proposals.**

- Both accused to be struck off and entered on the next list in the *Bulletin of Order*.
- Transfer to the Kessaar penal colony at the close of the inquiry.
- No public mention of the source of the intelligence. The press may refer to the “vigilance” of the service.

The undersigned remains at the Director-General’s disposal for any explanation and requests, in view of the family ties, that the inquiry be entrusted to another commissioner.
