## INSPECTION REPORT

### Subject: launch of the Tennok lifeboat in the night of 21 to 22 November 1924; loss of the lifeboat coxswain

Vellaak, 5 December 1924.

The undersigned inspector, having gone to the Cap de Tennok from 26 to 30 November, has the honour to report the following to the chief engineer.

**1. The facts.** In the night of 21 to 22 November, in a north-westerly gale, force 10 to 11, with a very high sea, the keeper of the light, Mr Jorre Haask, sighted a distress flare at about 1.30 on the reef of the Dents de Tennok. The alarm was given on the bell. The lifeboat left its house at 2.00, manned by eight men, under the command of its coxswain, **A. Varenque**, in service since 1906.

**2. The wrecked vessel.** A motor cutter of a dozen metres or so, bearing the name **Albatros** on her stern, with no registration number, no flag and no ship’s papers. The wreck, which the undersigned inspected at low tide on 27 November, still held twenty-two bales of Thalbère leaf **tobacco**, which the Customs have had seized. There is little doubt as to where the vessel came from or where she was bound.

**3. The rescue.** The lifeboat managed to come alongside the wreck in the lee of the reef. Three men were taken off. As the coxswain moved forward to pass one last line, a sea swept the lifeboat. **Coxswain A. Varenque, lost overboard**, could not be found despite an hour’s search in the surf and a second launch at dawn. The second coxswain, Mr Davik Laakvik, brought the lifeboat and the survivors back to the boathouse at 4.50. The crew showed courage and discipline beyond all praise.

**4. The survivors.** They were cared for at the keeper’s house, then questioned by the undersigned on 27 November. They state:

- **F. V., aged 24, of Tennok, claiming to be skipper** of the cutter;
- two deckhands, aged 19 and 31, of Vellaak, who refused to answer beyond giving their names and particulars.

The undersigned has thought it preferable to record here only the initials of those concerned. The first does not dispute the facts. At the close of his questioning he asked leave to stay with the family of the missing man “until the sea gives him back”. It was granted. The people of the cape, questioned in their turn, saw nothing and knew nothing, as is the custom.

**5. Proposals.**

- That the name of Coxswain **Aldrik Varenque** be put forward for the lifesavers’ gold medal, posthumously.
- That a pension be paid to his widow, Mrs Tenna Orrvik, postmistress at Tennok, without prejudice to her salary.
- That proceedings against the survivors be left to the discretion of the Customs, the undersigned being of the opinion, for his part, that the burden they bear exceeds any the court could inflict on them.
