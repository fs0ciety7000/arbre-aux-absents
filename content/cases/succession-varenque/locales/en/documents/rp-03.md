## THE PAPERMAKERS’ TRIAL

### Sitting of Tuesday 5 May 1953 — Examination of the accused Gaspard Varenque (extracts from the shorthand record)

*Presiding: Mr President Hubert Chaudanne. For the prosecution: Mr Advocate General Gerbault. For the defence: Maître Fernand Desaulnes.*

**THE PRESIDENT.** — Accused, stand up. Your surname, given names and occupation.

**THE ACCUSED.** — Varenque, Gaspard, Anselme. Born on 8 October 1898 at Moulinvert. **Director of the Varenque Paper Mills** since 1925, chairman of the company since 1938.

**THE PRESIDENT.** — You have heard the documents relating to the grey booklets contract read out. You do not dispute having signed them?

**THE ACCUSED.** — I dispute nothing that bears my signature.

**THE PRESIDENT.** — You knew what those booklets were used for?

**THE ACCUSED.** — I knew.

**THE PRESIDENT.** — And you went on supplying them until 1952.

**THE ACCUSED.** — Until 6 April 1952. The last delivery is entered in my books. I have nothing to add.

**THE ADVOCATE GENERAL.** — I should like to question the accused on another point. Mr Varenque, the accountants’ examination of your stores shows, between July 1944 and April 1952, outgoings of paper bearing a watermark that correspond to no invoice: some nine thousand sheets. Where did they go?

**THE ACCUSED.** — (*Silence.*)

**THE ADVOCATE GENERAL.** — I shall put the question another way. Did you sell that paper on the black market? To the Regency, outside the contract? To someone else?

**THE ACCUSED.** — I shall not answer that question.

**THE PRESIDENT.** — You realise, accused, that your silence may be held against you?

**THE ACCUSED.** — I realise it, Mr President.

**THE ADVOCATE GENERAL.** — Your sister, Odile Varenque, was arrested in October 1941 for making false papers on paper from your firm. Who denounced her?

**THE ACCUSED.** — (*Silence.*)

**THE ADVOCATE GENERAL.** — Your son was shot in 1944. Your sister died on Kessaar. Will you say nothing, at least before the families of the absent who are here in this courtroom?

**THE ACCUSED.** — I will not defend myself with the dead.

*(Stir in the courtroom. The President threatens to have the public gallery cleared.)*

**MAÎTRE DESAULNES.** — Mr President, my client has answered every question that bears on the charges. The rest is a matter for his conscience. I ask the court to record that the prosecution produces no document concerning the outgoings of paper, other than their absence from the books.

**THE PRESIDENT.** — It is so recorded. The sitting is adjourned.
