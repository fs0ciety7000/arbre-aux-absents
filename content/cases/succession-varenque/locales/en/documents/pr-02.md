**THE PORT-SÉNANE ECHO** — Maritime, commercial and advertising journal — Saturday 8 March 1902 — 5 sous

## OBITUARY

### Captain Firmin Varenque

The house flag of the **Orvelle Packet Company** has flown at half-mast on the quai des Messageries since Thursday. **Captain Firmin Varenque** died on Thursday morning, 6 March, at the seamen’s hospital of Port-Sénane, from a fever caught at sea on the way home from his last voyage. He was in his fifty-third year.

Born at Moulinvert, the son of a miller on the Clairette, nothing destined him for the sea except a taste which his father, as he once told us, “never understood and never fought”. He shipped as a cabin boy at Port-Sénane at fifteen. Second mate at twenty-three, he had been a **master mariner** since 1882 and commanded in turn, for the Company, the *Orvelle*, the *Duchesse-d’Ambre* and the *Velmarchois*. Passengers on our Golfe d’Ambre lines will remember a jovial man who knew the name of every ship’s boy and told stories at table that nobody was obliged to believe.

He wore in his left ear, in the manner of the seafaring men of his family, a gold ring which he laughingly called “the price of my grave, if the sea gives me back”. The sea did not take him: it was a fever caught ashore that carried him off, in a bed, which he would have found, one of his officers tells us, “thoroughly out of place”.

He leaves a widow at the Cap de Tennok, where he had taken a wife in 1873 and where he returned between voyages, and a son, a lifeboatman at Tennok. His brother, Mr Anselme Varenque, the papermaker of Moulinvert, arrived in Port-Sénane yesterday.

The body will be carried home by sea to Tennok, as the deceased wished. A mass will be said on Monday at ten o’clock in the church of Saint-Brice du Port, for the officers and crews of the Company.

*Our engraving is taken from a photograph of the captain made in 1895 aboard the* Velmarchois*, kindly lent by the Company.*
