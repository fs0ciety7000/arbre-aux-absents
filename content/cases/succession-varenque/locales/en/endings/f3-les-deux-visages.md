## [commun] The sitting

On 15 November 1974, the Re-registration Commission settled the table of descent for file
No. 74-0318. Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel were re-registered posthumously; Lise Daubry's
parentage was restored: born on Kessaar on 3 March 1942, to Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel. Les
Tilleuls was divided among the established heirs: Blanche Daubry, Selda Varenque, Armand Varenque,
Hélène Marsal, Lise Daubry and Tavik Varenque.

But what the press took away from the sitting was page fourteen of the report.

## [le-delateur] The informer

The report named the author of the denunciation of October 1941: Marcel Daubry, Commissioner of
Registers, the engraver's brother-in-law, who had noticed the acid burns on her hands and had her
followed. It also named the man who had the Grisenoue registers burnt in April 1952, and the man who
had died in Brenzac in 1959 under another name. They were one and the same.

At Bains-de-Mérande, Blanche Daubry, seventy-three years old, asked to see the report. She read it
in the reading room, without spectacles. Then she asked Lise to stay her daughter all the same.
Lise stayed.

## [le-papetier] The papermaker

The report further established that Gaspard Varenque, director of the Paper Mills, the Regency's
supplier of paper for the grey booklets, had, from July 1944 to April 1952, passed to the Fern
network nearly nine thousand sheets of paper bearing the “undated fern” watermark, through a fishing
skipper from Vellaak. He had told no one. At his trial in 1953 he had refused to use it: “I will
not defend myself with the dead.”

The blue register came back to the Archives on 20 November, left at the front desk of the Palais
des Chartes by a fair-haired lady who did not leave her name, but whom the hall keeper recognised,
because she had said good evening to him on television for six years. Mathilde Varenque had spoken
to her husband. Armand Varenque never said what she had told him. He withdrew his objection in a
four-line letter.

On 30 November, *The Aubrelieu Courier* published, under Paul Marsal's name, “The Regency Papers
(7): the papermaker and the Gull”. It was a correction. It began: “I was wrong, and I misled my
readers for six years.”

On 2 December, Hélène Marsal took a day's leave, which she had not done since 1968, and went to lay
flowers at the Moulinvert vault. She did not know which flowers her father had liked. She took
ferns.

## [S3] The Washerwoman

Claude Sauge typed “Lucien Varenque” on the Reader's keyboard and learnt that she was descended from
a poster. The acknowledgement of 1917 having been upheld, she was added to the heirs and hung *The
Washerwoman* above the console.

## [S4] The lifeboat plaque

At Tennok, Tavik Varenque had the lifeboat plaque engraved: “Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, died saving
his son”. The same month, without either of them knowing, Paul Marsal wrote in his correction that
the Gull had spent his life paying back one November night. Tavik cut out the article.

## [S6] The last letter

Between two sheets of the herbarium, an envelope with no reference was waiting for “the
rapporteur”. *If you are reading this, you have found two documents. That is all I wanted.*

## [fin] End

The Varenque collection went back to the stacks, sixty-three articles, complete. On the record card
for article 41, the workshop added a note: *original returned to the collection 20 November 1974*.
Lise Daubry typed it herself, then stood for a while in front of the register, without opening it,
looking at the blue cover.
