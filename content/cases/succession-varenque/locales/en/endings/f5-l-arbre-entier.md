## [commun] The sitting

On 15 November 1974, the Re-registration Commission sat for three hours on file No. 74-0318, which
had never been seen before. The report ran to ninety-one pages and to two documents per line.
Maître Armand Varenque made no plea: he had withdrawn his objection on 13 November, the day after a
fair-haired lady had left at the front desk of the Palais des Chartes a register with a blue cover,
wrapped in a scarf.

The Commission re-registered Orrin Kessvel posthumously. It set aside the death certificate drawn up
on Kessaar on 14 February 1943 and re-registered Odile Varenque, living, resident in Vellaak. It
restored the parentage of Lise Daubry, born on Kessaar on 3 March 1942, to Odile Varenque and Orrin
Kessvel. It recognised the deed lodged in 1917 with Maître Brunesseau: Victor Sauge was the son of
Lucien Varenque, and his daughter Claude an heir. It named Marcel Daubry as the author of the 1941
denunciation. It established that from 1944 to 1952 Gaspard Varenque had supplied the Fern network
with the paper for its forgeries. It recorded that the shipwrecked men of the *Albatros* were Firmin
Varenque and his crew, and that Aldrik Varenque had died saving his son. Lastly it recorded, in a
single sentence, that Miss Adélaïde Varenque, schoolmistress, had organised her niece's escape in
1943, with the keeper Teodor Haask and a fishing skipper from Vellaak, and that the Commission could
see no article of law under which to reproach her for it.

The legacy was divided into eight shares: Blanche, Selda, Armand, Hélène, Lise, Tavik, Claude and
Odile.

## [ce-qui-suivit] What followed

Paul Marsal published a correction under his own name, on the front page, and Hélène Marsal laid
ferns at the Moulinvert vault. Mathilde Varenque never told anyone what she had said to her husband;
nor did Armand, but since then he has worn his bow ties a little less tight.

Blanche Daubry read the report without spectacles, then asked Lise to stay her daughter all the
same. Lise stayed, and introduced her to her mother.

Claude Sauge typed “Lucien Varenque” on the Reader's keyboard, laughed out loud in the reading
room, which earned her a stern look from the archivist, and hung *The Washerwoman* above the
console.

At Tennok, the lifeboat plaque now reads: “Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, died saving his son”.

Between two sheets of the herbarium, an envelope with no reference was waiting for the rapporteur.
*If you are reading this, you have found two documents. That is all I wanted.*

## [les-tilleuls-21-juin-1975] Les Tilleuls, 21 June 1975

It was Selda who had the idea, and Odile who set the camera on the tripod. They had come from
everywhere: from Vellaak by the launch, from Tennok on foot and then by car, from Bains-de-Mérande
by coach, from Port-Sénane, from Aubrelieu. It was hot. The limes were in flower, for once on the
right date.

On the front steps, where a wedding had been photographed in 1922, they arranged themselves as best
they could. Blanche in the middle, in an armchair she had insisted on. Selda beside her, an unlit
cigarillo in her hand, because Blanche had insisted on that too. Tavik behind, a head taller than
everyone and not knowing what to do with his hands. Claude, arms folded, with that eye of hers that
looked slightly to one side. Hélène and Paul, arm in arm. Armand and Mathilde, he in a bow tie, she
in sunglasses. Lise, without a headband. And an empty chair at the end of the row, such as has been
left at family meals since 1953, for the absent.

Odile adjusted the focus, checked the light, then turned to the person standing a little apart, on
the drive, with a file under one arm, brought along out of habit.

“Do you know how to work this?” she asked, holding out the camera.

Then she went and sat in the empty chair.

## [la-derniere-piece] The last document

The Varenque collection now numbers sixty-four articles. The last, referenced FV 64, is a group
photograph, a silver print, 24 × 18 cm: ten people on the front steps of Les Tilleuls, on 21 June
1975. On the back, two hands have written down the names, all of them, without leaving one out, and
without a tear.

The photographer's name does not appear on it. It is in file 74-0318.
