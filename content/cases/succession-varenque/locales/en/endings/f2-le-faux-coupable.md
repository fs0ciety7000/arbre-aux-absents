title: "The False Culprit"
---

## [commun] The sitting

On 15 November 1974, the Re-registration Commission settled the table of descent for file
No. 74-0318. Every name stood on two documents; none was contested. Odile Varenque and Orrin
Kessvel were re-registered posthumously. Lise Daubry's parentage was restored: born on Kessaar on
3 March 1942, to Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel. Les Tilleuls was divided among the established
heirs: Blanche Daubry, Selda Varenque, Armand Varenque, Hélène Marsal, Lise Daubry and Tavik
Varenque.

The report, for its part, went further than the tree. To the question “Who denounced the Fern
network?”, it answered: Gaspard Varenque, director of the Paper Mills, supplier of the grey
booklets, the engraver's brother. The documents cited were public; they said what everyone already
believed.

## [ce-que-chacun-en-fit] What each of them made of it

On 2 December Paul Marsal published “The Regency Papers (7): what the report says”. He no longer
needed to write between the lines: a public office was writing it for him. Hélène Marsal cut out
the article and never read it again.

Maître Armand Varenque sued *The Aubrelieu Courier* for libel, in the name of his father's memory.
The case was adjourned three times. It has not been heard. Mathilde Varenque has stopped mentioning
it at table; she says the table has nothing to do with it.

At the conservation workshop, Lise Daubry received a copy of the report, as a document to be added
to the collection. She read it once. In a drawer she had the reel MF-74-112, which she had
microfilmed herself in September and which nobody had asked her about since. She put the report in
its box, the box on its shelf, and said nothing to anyone. It was not her file. It was her family,
but it was not her file.

## [Q3 fourni] Two lines that do not agree

Three pages further on, the report also said that from 1944 to 1952 Gaspard Varenque had supplied
the network with the paper bearing a watermark for its forged papers. Nobody noticed that the two
statements sat badly together. A man who gives up his sister in 1941 and arms his friends in 1944:
the Commission wrote “change of heart”. Paul Marsal wrote “remorse”. Nobody wrote “mistake”.

## [S3] The Washerwoman

Claude Sauge, who was checking a tape from the Central Indexed Collection, typed “Lucien Varenque”
on the Reader's keyboard one evening and learnt that she was descended from a soap advertisement.
The acknowledgement of 1917 having been upheld, she was added to the heirs. She hung *The
Washerwoman* above the console. It is the only thing in the whole file that made her laugh.

## [S4] The lifeboat plaque

At Tennok, Tavik Varenque had the lifeboat plaque engraved: “Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, died saving
his son”. In Vellaak, nobody asked him which son.

## [S6] The last letter

Between two sheets of the herbarium, an envelope with no reference was waiting for “the
rapporteur”. *If you are reading this, you have found two documents. That is all I wanted.* It
reached office 12 after the closure. It was added to the file, which was closed, and which was
wrong on one point.

## [fin] End

The tree was right. Thirty names, thirty faces, thirty occupations, each on two documents. The rest
of the report was not, and the tree says nothing about it: it has no slot for the guilty.

In the Moulinvert vault, Gaspard Varenque's plaque still bears his name and his dates, and nothing
else. Nobody leaves flowers there. Now and then the wind blows lime leaves against it.
