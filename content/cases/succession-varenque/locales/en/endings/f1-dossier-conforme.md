## [commun] The sitting

On 15 November 1974, at ten o'clock, the Re-registration Commission examined file No. 74-0318.
The sitting lasted forty minutes, which is long for the Commission and short for four
generations. Maître Armand Varenque argued inadmissibility, then privacy, then nothing: the table
of descent was complete, and every name stood on two documents. The Commission stamped it.

Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel were re-registered posthumously in the registers of Moulinvert and
of Vellaak, with the note “declared absent by the Regency in November 1941; restored by decision
of 15 November 1974”. Lise Daubry's parentage was restored: born on Kessaar on 3 March 1942, to
Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel. The 1942 adoption judgment was not set aside; a line was added in
the margin. In Velmarche, since 1973, a great many lines have been added in margins.

Les Tilleuls and the shares in the Paper Mills were divided among the established heirs: Blanche
Daubry, Selda Varenque, Armand Varenque, Hélène Marsal, Lise Daubry and Tavik Varenque. Each of them
received a share of a house where most of them had never slept.

## [S3] The Washerwoman

One Monday in December, Claude Sauge, who was checking a tape from the Central Indexed Collection,
typed on the Reader's keyboard a name she had read in the report: “Lucien Varenque”. The printer
produced eleven references. The ninth was a poster. She requested the document, looked at it for a
long time, and told the duty archivist that she was descended, apparently, from a soap advertisement
and a washerwoman who laughed. The acknowledgement of 1917 having been upheld, Claude Sauge was added
to the heirs. She had a reproduction of *The Washerwoman* framed and hung it above the Reader's
console, where it still is; the readers think it is decoration.

## [S2] The undated paper

The report mentioned, in a single line, that from 1944 to 1952 Gaspard Varenque had supplied the
Fern network with the paper for its forged papers. It did not say who had denounced the network,
for want of a document. Nobody picked up the line, except Hélène Marsal, who copied it out by hand
in her appointment book, between two patients, and told nobody why.

## [S4] The lifeboat plaque

At Tennok, Tavik Varenque had a new plaque engraved for the lifeboat house. The old one read:
“Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, lost at sea on 22 November 1924 saving three shipwrecked men”. The new
one reads: “Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, died saving his son”. The Vellaak letter-cutter asked whether
he should add the son's name. Tavik said no: everyone here knew.

## [S5] The informer's name

The public report named Marcel Daubry, Commissioner of Registers, as the author of the denunciation
of the Fern network in October 1941. At Bains-de-Mérande, Blanche Daubry, seventy-three years old,
asked to see the report. She read it through, in the reading room, without her spectacles, because
she refused to wear them in front of strangers. Then she asked Lise, who had come with her, to stay
her daughter all the same. Lise stayed.

## [S6] The last letter

When the workshop put the collection back in boxes, Lise Daubry found, slipped between two sheets of
Clémence's herbarium, an envelope with no reference, addressed “To the rapporteur”. The handwriting
was round, the ink violet. *If you are reading this, you have found two documents. That is all I
wanted.* She had it taken to office 12 without opening it. Well, almost without.

## [retour-aux-magasins] Back to the stacks

The Varenque collection went back to the stacks of the Palais des Chartes, series FV, sixty-three
articles, less one. File 74-0318 was closed as in order.

On 3 December a letter reached the Office, posted in Vellaak, typed on a machine whose *e* skipped.
It said only: “Ke p looking.” It was added to the file, which was closed.
