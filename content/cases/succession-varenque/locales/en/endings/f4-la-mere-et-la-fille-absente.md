title: "Mother and Daughter (Absent)"

## [commun] The sitting

On 15 November 1974, the Re-registration Commission settled the table of descent for file
No. 74-0318. Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel were re-registered posthumously. Lise Daubry's
parentage was restored: born on Kessaar on 3 March 1942, to Odile Varenque and Orrin Kessvel. Les
Tilleuls was divided among the established heirs: Blanche Daubry, Selda Varenque, Armand Varenque,
Hélène Marsal, Lise Daubry and Tavik Varenque.

The public report said nothing about Vellaak. The rapporteur had chosen to follow an instruction
written in 1962 on undated paper: *If one day someone comes looking, let them leave me absent, but
let whoever it is be told.*

## [le-paquet] The parcel

On 2 December, Lise Daubry found on her table at the conservation workshop a parcel wrapped in
brown paper, with no sender, left by the Office's post room. Inside were a copy of *Faces of the
Coast*, the catalogue of the 1964 exhibition, and an Office record card, unsigned, with a reference
and a single line: *For you, and for nobody else.*

The catalogue lay open at the plate “For É., 1962”: an empty cradle in a photographer's studio,
beside a window looking out on a fjord. Lise looked at the plate for a long time. Then she turned
the pages to the self-portrait of 1958, laid her gloved hand on the paper, above the forelock, and
took off her headband.

She asked for three days' leave, the first since she had joined the workshop.

## [S2] The undated paper

The report established that from 1944 to 1952 Gaspard Varenque had supplied the network with
“undated fern” paper. Lise Daubry, who had examined that paper as an expert, filed the report beside
the microfilm MF-74-112. She now knew what paper her mother had written her letters on.

## [S3] The Washerwoman

Claude Sauge typed “Lucien Varenque” on the Reader's keyboard and learnt that she was descended from
a poster. The acknowledgement of 1917 having been upheld, she was added to the heirs and hung *The
Washerwoman* above the console.

## [S4] The lifeboat plaque

At Tennok, Tavik Varenque had the lifeboat plaque engraved: “Aldrik Varenque, coxswain, died saving
his son”. On the day it was put up, there was a woman in an Ostrian jersey on the jetty, with a
camera. She did not take a photograph.

## [S5] The informer's name

The public report named Marcel Daubry as the author of the 1941 denunciation. Blanche Daubry asked
to see the report, read it without spectacles, then asked Lise to stay her daughter all the same.
Lise said yes. She did not tell her where she was going the next day.

## [S6] The last letter

Between two sheets of the herbarium, an envelope with no reference was waiting for “the
rapporteur”. *If you are reading this, you have found two documents. That is all I wanted.*

## [fin] End

The Port-Sénane launch puts in at Vellaak at eleven o'clock, when it is not late. That day it was
not. A woman of thirty-two with light chestnut hair and a white forelock above her forehead stepped
onto the quay with a small suitcase and an exhibition catalogue under her arm. She asked a fisherman
where the Ilvess studio was.

The fisherman pointed out the jetée Est, the drying shed, a small-paned window with a fern in a pot.
Then he took a better look at her, and said nothing more.

The register, for its part, still says that Odile Varenque died on Kessaar in 1943. Nobody in
Vellaak will correct it.
