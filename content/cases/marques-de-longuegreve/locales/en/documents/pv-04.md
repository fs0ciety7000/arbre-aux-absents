**RECORD OF HEARING**

On Thursday 13 October 1977, at nine o'clock in the morning, at the Office, Aubrelieu, before us,
permanent rapporteur, assisted by the clerk, there appeared on summons:

**Mrs Aline Lestaud**, aged fifty-two, ornithologist at the Nature Reserves Service, author of the 1976
note attached to the petition of the State Lands.

“My father, Clément Lestaud, was a sailor with the Packet Company; he died in 1950. He didn't talk about
the island. I knew he was born ‘in the gulf’, that's all. My grandfather, Fulbert Lestaud, gathered
seaweed, I believe; I never knew him.”

“It was only in September, from the notice in the paper and from the Friendly Society, that I learned
my family came from Longuegrève. I myself wrote the note that is being used to take it away from you.”

“I ringed terns for fifteen years on my great-grandfather's strip. I didn't know it. I don't know
whether that changes anything for the reserve. For me, it does.”

She hands over the 1961 and 1976 photographs from the Nature Reserves Service.

Having heard this read over, she confirms it and signs.

::signature[A. Lestaud — the rapporteur — the clerk]
