*Double sheet of exercise paper, written in black ink in Father Rabier's hand. Attached: the notary's

requisition, in view of the legatee's filiation.*

## Declaration

This day, Sunday the sixteenth of February eighteen hundred and ninety, on the island of Longuegrève,
in the absence of a notary, the island having none, we, Fortuné Rabier, officiating priest, received at
his bedside the declaration of **Siméon Cormaud**, shipwright, head of the Cormaud house, sick in body
and sound in mind, in the presence of four witnesses of the island.

Who told us, in his own speech, what we write down as closely as we can:

“My two boys have been in the cemetery since the westerly gale. Noël, the child the sea gave me, is my
son before God. He has my boat, he has my trade, he will have the Cormaud strip and the share of shore
that goes with it. I say it before the four houses so that nothing is taken back from him.”

## Note of deposit

“Deposited at the office by Father Rabier on Monday 3 March 1890; the testator died on 1 March. Shore
testament, without form, valid as holograph? — No: not written in the testator's hand. Valid as a deed
received by a minister of religion in an isolated place, under the Grand-Ducal code, if the legatee
comes forward within the year. Neither registered nor entered, the legatee not having come forward. —
A. Lhermet.”

*In the margin, in another hand, 1977:* “The legatee, Noël Cormaud, was never notified: nothing in the
index. — G. Lhermet, notary.”
