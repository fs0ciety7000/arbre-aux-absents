::stamp[CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILE 77-0874 — OBJECT DESCRIBED]

*Investigator's description, after the sexton's survey and Mrs Marest's notebook.*

Bronze bell, 0.58 m in diameter, hanging since 1908 in the small bay of the belfry of the Môle church,
in Port-Sénane; it now tolls only for the dead of the Cabanes.

On the waist, in capitals: “LONGUEGRÈVE 1868”. Below, in relief, four cast figures, side by side, each
the height of a finger: a fork with three tines, a diamond-shaped eye, a chevron-shaped arrow, a
gallows. The sexton calls them “the four heads”: the Fork, the Eye, the Arrow and the Gallows, as they
say at the Cabanes.

On the opposite face, in a cartouche, a figure that unites the four heads on a single foot, the same as
on the 1868 cadastre plan: ::mark[lg-portrait-e5dfe7]. Below: “Lemerle Foundry, Aubrelieu”.

*Investigator's note:* “This is a founder's engraving, after the drawing of the surveyor Constant
Mérel. It is nobody's mark.”
