| LG-64-02 | A and B | Saturday 22 August 1964 | Les Cabanes d'Ambre, Mrs Cormaud's kitchen | Victoire Cormaud, née Oyard, aged 71 | 31'45 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. Recorded in the afternoon, following tape 01.*

[00'10] **MAREST.** You were fifteen in 1908.

[00'14] **VICTOIRE.** Fifteen in February. The winter before had been bad, no worse than others. Elzéar's
little Anne, my brother's girl, died of the croup at Christmas, four years old. My mother nursed her
three nights. Croup, we knew what that was, my mother was a midwife, she'd seen plenty. And then in
February Clément's little ones had the measles, both of them, spots all over. They got over it. And my
father died at the beginning of March, of his chest. He'd been coughing for ten years.

[02'40] **MAREST.** And the fever?

[02'42] **VICTOIRE.** What fever? *[silence]* That's what they wrote afterwards. We never saw any. The
doctor came on the Saturday, with the Company's launch. A grey man, kind, in a hurry. He looked at the
throats of Clément's children, he looked at old Jacquette, my mother, who told him what she thought of
Grand-Terre doctors, and he said: nothing. He said “nothing”, I heard him. He went back in the
afternoon.

[05'15] **MAREST.** And then?

[05'18] **VICTOIRE.** Then the constables. With a paper. They read out to us that we had to leave on 2
April, that the houses would be burned because of the sickness, and that Mr Garnaud was housing us at
the Cabanes. They called us by the list, house by house: the evacuation list, the constables had it.
Everyone had to make their mark or sign as they went aboard. Me, I signed. I was proud, if you please.
*[short laugh]*

[08'02] **MAREST.** Did everyone leave on the 2nd?

[08'05] **VICTOIRE.** Everyone but old Josse. Josse Goémard, head of the Fork, my mother's uncle… no, her
brother. He sat down on his stone, in front of his door, and he said he wouldn't go aboard. The
constables didn't dare. He was old, he had his knife at his belt, like all of them.

[09'30] **MAREST.** And how did he come?

[09'33] **VICTOIRE.** The next day. Mr Garnaud went to fetch him himself, with his launch. They got to the
jetty about midday. I was there, we were all there, looking at the island. Mr Garnaud helped him down,
and on the quay he talked to him. A long time, very low. He talked to him like to a dog, or like to a
brother, I don't know. Old Josse answered nothing. He looked at his feet.

[12'20] **MAREST.** Had you seen Mr Garnaud before?

[12'23] **VICTOIRE.** At the Company, from a distance. He was the boss. *[silence]* My mother held me by the
arm that day, very tight. I don't know why.

[13'40] **MAREST.** And the burning?

[13'42] **VICTOIRE.** The Saturday. We saw it from the jetty: on a clear day you can see the island. First
one smoke, then several, then the big one, the chapel's, because it had a tarred roof. The women were
crying. The mistress wasn't crying. She had her exercise book against her, as she always had for a
month.

*[14'30 – 31'45: Mrs Cormaud describes the settling in at the Cabanes, the sheds, the first wages at the
Company. See summary of side B in the notebook.]*
