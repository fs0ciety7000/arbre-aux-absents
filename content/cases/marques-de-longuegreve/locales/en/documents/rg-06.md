::stamp[CADASTRE OF THE ISLANDS OF THE GULF — SHEET 4 — LONGUEGRÈVE]

*Photographic reproduction of the original plan, wash and ink, 62 × 48 cm. Scale of 1,000 toises.
North at the top.*

## What the plan shows

The island is long and low, lying from north to south. Four bands of land run across it from east to west,
from the cliff to the shore: these are the **four strips** of the 1868 roll, each tinted a colour and
numbered. From north to south:

| Lot | Strip | What is on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Lestaud Lubin | the pointe du Feu (added in pencil, in another hand: “light (1872)”); the common oven |
| 1 | Goémard Gervais | the hamlet (fourteen houses shown as small squares); the well |
| 2 | Oyard Clovis | the chapel, the cemetery, the boat slip |
| 3 | Cormaud Siméon | the boatyard; the salt meadows |

To the west, along the whole length of the island, a dotted band of sand: “The Longue Grève”. The
surveyor has extended the boundaries of the four lots in ink down to the low-water mark: each house's
share of the shore. To the east, the cliff and the path to the slip.

## The cartouche

Bottom right, in a double-ruled cartouche: “Cadastre of the Islands — Sheet 4 — Longuegrève — Surveyed
on 7 July 1868 — C. Mérel, surveyor”. Above the name, the surveyor has drawn a figure that he calls in
his key “the mark of the island”: ::mark[lg-portrait-e5dfe7].

*Surveyor's key, in his own hand:* “The four houses of the island each have a sign: the Fork for
Goémard, the Eye for Oyard, the Arrow for Cormaud, the Gallows for Lestaud. I have brought the four
signs together on a single foot, to ornament the cartouche. The island people make no use of it.”
