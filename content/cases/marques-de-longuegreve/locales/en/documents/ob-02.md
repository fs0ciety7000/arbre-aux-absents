::stamp[CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILE 77-0874 — INVENTORY OF BOX No. 108]

*Inventory drawn up by the investigator at the Port-Sénane Savings Bank, in the presence of the
representatives of the four houses.*

**Four caskets** of oak, identical, 30 × 20 × 15 cm, brass fittings, no lock. On each lid, a mark carved
with a knife, in a careful stroke:

| Casket | Mark | Opened by | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ::mark[lg-portrait-9e0e20] | Mr Félix Goémard | sixteen folded papers, each round a piece of raw amber |
| 2 | ::mark[lg-portrait-aa93f2] | Mrs Rose Pellerin | sixteen papers, sixteen pieces; one holds a whole insect |
| 3 | ::mark[lg-portrait-5ce728] | Mr Marin Cormaud | sixteen papers, sixteen pieces |
| 4 | ::mark[lg-portrait-8084e7] | Mrs Aline Lestaud | sixteen papers, sixteen pieces |

Each paper bears a date in the same careful hand: “westerly gale of 9 November 1912”, “westerly gale of
2 February 1915”… The first is from the winter of 1908–1909, the last from the winter of 1922–1923. The
paper wrapping the piece with the insect, in the casket of the Eye, says: “For the Eye. The finest of
1915.”

The pieces range from the size of a walnut to that of a fist. The largest, in each casket, are of Shore
quality, flawless.

*Observation:* Mrs Pellerin held the piece with the insect up to the light from the window. She said:
“My father picked that up, and he sold it. And they kept it for him.”
