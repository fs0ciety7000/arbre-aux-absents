::stamp[REPUBLIC OF VELMARCHE — CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILIATIONS DEPARTMENT]

Aubrelieu, Monday 3 October 1977

**Subject:** file No. 77-0874 — petition of the State Lands for the reconstruction of the filiations
of the owners of the island of Longuegrève (Act of 14 May 1973, Art. 15).

**To:** permanent rapporteur, office 12, third floor.

**Enclosures:** items 2 to 8 (see schedule at the end of this note).

Dear Rapporteur,

Here is a file in which nobody signs. You have handled two in which people lied with signatures;
this one will teach you to read people who told the truth with a knife.

## 1. The petition

The State Lands Administration wishes to expropriate the island of Longuegrève, in the Golfe
d'Ambre, to create a nature reserve there (item 2). The island has been uninhabited since the 1908
evacuation; in the cadastre it still belongs to the four houses that lived on it, as entered in the
**1868 roll**: Goémard, Oyard, Cormaud and Lestaud, one strip each. To expropriate, one must
compensate; to compensate, one must know whom. The island's chapel register, the only complete
civil record of the population, was burned in 1908 by act of public authority. This is precisely the
case of Article 15: the Office reconstructs the filiations on two concordant documents.

## 2. The objection

The Amber Company, which has worked the island's shores since 1908, formed an objection
on 22 September through Maître Valère Joubaud (item 3). It pleads possession and the validity of the
1908 health certificate, and maintains that “a mark is not a signature”. The president of the
Company, Miss Edmée Garnaud, asks to be heard.

## 3. The memorandum and the offer

The Cabanes Friendly Society, which brings together at the Cabanes d'Ambre the evacuated families and
their descendants, lodged a memorandum on 26 September (item 4). It does not ask for the
compensation; it asks for the island.

On 28 September, Mrs Ginette Sollier, of Bains-de-Mérande, wrote to tell us that she held some
exercise books left by her great-aunt, a schoolmistress on the island (item 7). I advise you to answer
her quickly: people who offer their papers change their minds when kept waiting.

## 4. Your assignment

You will draw up the table of the **four heads of the 1868 roll**, their wives, those of their children
who held the house or founded a line, and **one living representative per branch**. The other
beneficiaries appear in the Friendly Society's annex; the Commission will refer to it.

The rules are those of the house. A slot is established only on **two** concordant **documents**. The
occupation retained is the one for which the person is **known in at least two documents**: neither a
day's employment nor what rumour says.

Article 15, paragraph 2, adds a rule you have never applied: “the mark affixed in his own hand by a
person unable to sign is worth a signature when it is recognised on two documents”. Consequently,
**for the islanders who never signed, the table retains the mark instead of the portrait**. The table
will tell you, slot by slot, what it expects. A mark copied by a clerk or engraved by a craftsman is
not a mark: it is a drawing.

## 5. What is established

One slot already is, on the strength of the University's Marest collection (items 5 and 8): that of
**Mrs Victoire Cormaud, née Victoire Oyard** (1893–1970), amber sorter at the Company from 1908 to
1955, whom the 1964 investigator called “the last voice of Longuegrève”. It was she who taught her to
read the marks. She will teach you too, in writing.

## 6. The timetable

The joint Office–State Lands Commission sits on **Monday 14 November**. Hearings will be called as the
table progresses; the archivist will bring up the next boxes only on sight of your first stamps. That
is the custom, and it is also the only means I have found of making you read in order.

Finally, I would remind you that Longuegrève is not a matter of figures. The State Lands offer a round
sum; the Friendly Society asks for the right to be buried at home. You will not decide between them:
you will say who they are.
