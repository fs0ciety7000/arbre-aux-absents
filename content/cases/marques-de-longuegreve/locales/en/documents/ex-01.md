::stamp[CIVIL RE-REGISTRATION OFFICE — FILE 77-0874 — EXPERT REPORT]

**Subject:** comparison of the Fork-shaped marks with a right-hand notch found in the file; incidentally,
the mark of the Lestaud bride in the marriage entry of 1877.

**Documents examined:** roll of ship's boys of the Maritime Enrolment (1871); safe-deposit register of
the Savings Bank (1908); sealed letter of 1908; holograph testament of 1921; photographs of the
fireplace in the Hôtel Garnaud (1898) and of the grave in Port-Sénane cemetery (1924); Lestaud–Oyard
marriage of 1877, original and duplicate.

## 1. The four marks drawn by hand

| Document | Date | Mark | Observations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roll of ship's boys, fo. 214 | 1871 | ::mark[lg-portrait-480ba1] | official pen, faded ink; hesitant stroke of a boy who cannot hold a pen |
| Safe-deposit register, fo. 41 | 1908 | ::mark[lg-portrait-480ba1] | black ink, sure stroke |
| Sealed letter | 1908 | ::mark[lg-portrait-480ba1] | beside the initial “B.” |
| Holograph testament | 1921 | ::mark[lg-portrait-480ba1] | beside the signature “B. Garnaud” |

In all four documents the mark is drawn **by the same hand**: the foot is drawn from bottom to top, in a
single stroke; the left branch of the Fork is always longer than the right; the notch, on the right, is
pressed hard, with a slight hook at the end of the stroke. The gesture has grown firmer over fifty years;
it has not changed. I have never seen two islanders make their mark the same way.

## 2. The two carved marks

The mark on the fireplace (1898) and the one on the grave (1924) were **carved by other hands**, on the
model of the former: a professional stonecutter for the first, an amber cutter for the second (grinding
tool). They do not prove the hand; they prove the will.

## 3. Incidentally: the 1877 entry

On the original, the bride drew her own maiden mark: ::mark[lg-portrait-ea5469]. On the court
duplicate, the clerk copied a cross in its place: ::mark[lg-portrait-3f2b8f]. The cross is not an
island mark; it is nobody's mark.

## Conclusion

The ship's boy of 1871, entered as “Blaise, known as Garnaud, son of Gervais Goémard”, and the merchant
Blaise Garnaud of 1908 and 1921 made the same mark, with the same hand. They are the same man.

According to the grammar that Mrs Victoire Cormaud taught me, this mark is that of the second son of the
Goémard house at the time of the 1868 roll. It could have belonged to no one else.
