## The Cabanes want their island

*The Civil Re-registration Office has been examining the Longuegrève file since 3 October. At the
Cabanes, they refuse the State Lands' money.*

“We don't want the money. We want our dead.” Mr Marin Cormaud, fifty-seven, **foreman** at the
Company's workshop and president of the Cabanes Friendly Society, does not raise his voice. On 26
September the Friendly Society lodged a memorandum with the Office: it asks that the island remain with
the four houses, and that the descendants may be buried in Longuegrève cemetery, where no one has been
laid in the ground since 1908.

Beside him, Mr Félix Goémard, sixty-five, **fishing skipper**, owner of the *Saint-Gervais*, is
briefer: “My father was born there. My grandfather didn't want to leave it. That's reason enough.”

We spoke to **Miss Edmée Garnaud**, president of the Amber Company, which holds the rights to the
shores and has formed an objection to the petition. “My grandfather gave these families a roof, and
work for seventy years. I don't see what he is being blamed for.” Miss Garnaud's hearing by the Office
took place on Tuesday; she did not wish to say more.

A first commission is to sit on 14 November.
