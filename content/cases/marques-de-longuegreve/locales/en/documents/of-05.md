## The marks of Longuegrève

On Longuegrève, nobody signed: you *made your mark*. With a knife on oars, crates, spindles, bread
stamps, lintels and crosses; with a pen, shakily, in the registers of the Grand-Terre. A mark is a
**foot** (an upright stroke) and a **head** (the figure at the top). It tells the house, the rank, the
daughter, the wife. **It tells neither the age nor the given name.**

You receive your mark *at your first amber*, at about twelve. The children who went to the island's
school sign: they no longer need a mark, even if they still carve one on their oar.

### 1. The head is the house

Four heads, in straight strokes, “because a knife won't make a round”:

- the **Fork** (two branches in a V): house **Goémard**;
- the **Eye** (a closed diamond set on the foot): house **Oyard**;
- the **Arrow** (a chevron, point upwards, capping the foot): house **Cormaud**;
- the **Gallows** (an arm that goes off to the right from the top of the foot): house **Lestaud**.

### 2. The plain foot is the head of the house

In 1868, for the surveyor, the four heads “made their new marks”: the head alone, the foot plain.
Victoire says “the full mark”. Everything is read from them; the old crosses from before, nobody
reads them any more.

### 3. The son: the father's mark and a notch

The son takes his father's whole mark and adds beneath it a **notch**, a small stroke on one side
only of the foot: **on the left for the eldest, on the right for the second son**. “The third had no
strip, he went off without a mark.” One notch per generation: you read them from top to bottom, like a
path.

### 4. The daughter: the father's mark and a grain

Until she marries, the daughter marks as her father does, with a **grain** (a hollowed dot) to the
right of the head: **one for the elder, two for the younger**. No notch for daughters.

### 5. The wife: the husband's mark and a heel

Once married, she takes her husband's whole mark and carves **under the foot** a small head of the
house she was born into: the **heel**. She keeps it as a widow. Victoire: “My mother had my father's
Eye, and her little Fork underneath, because she was born a Goémard.”

### 6. The sea child

“The child the sea gives, you **bar its foot**: it is of the house, not of the blood.” The bar is a
slanting stroke that crosses the foot under the head, in place of the notch. Its sons put their
notches under the bar; its wife takes her heel like the others. On its own, without a head, the bar is
the **sea mark**: it is carved on the cross of a drowned man nobody could name.

### 7. What is not a mark

A mark is what the person drew or carved themselves, or what their own people carved on their cross:
“you carve on the dead man the mark he used to make”. What a clerk copies, what a founder engraves,
what a surveyor draws to look pretty, that is only a drawing.
