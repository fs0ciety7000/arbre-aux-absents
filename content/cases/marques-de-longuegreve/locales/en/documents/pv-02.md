**RECORD OF HEARING**

On Wednesday 12 October 1977, at ten o'clock in the morning, in the shed of the Cabanes Friendly
Society, before us, permanent rapporteur, assisted by the clerk, there appeared:

**Mr Marin Cormaud**, aged fifty-seven, foreman at the Company's workshop, president of the Friendly
Society; and **Mr Félix Goémard**, aged sixty-five, fishing skipper, owner of the *Saint-Gervais*.

## Mr Marin Cormaud

“My father, Lazare Cormaud, was a fisherman. My mother is Victoire, you know her. My grandfather is Noël
Cormaud, shipwright; they called him the sea child, because the sea had brought him. He used to say he
had two fathers, Siméon and the sea.”

He shows his grandfather's oar (item 28) and the lintel of the light house (item 29). He hands over the
Friendly Society's album, with the photograph of his parents' wedding.

## Mr Félix Goémard

“My father, Gatien Goémard, was an amber cutter at the workshop; he had his right thumb cut off, the
grinding-wheel accident, in 1910. My mother, Léontine Sauvat, was a seamstress, from Port-Sénane. My
grandfather Josse died the winter they arrived; I never knew him.”

He hands over his photographs, including one of the island cemetery: “The lady from the University had
given me a print, in '64.”

Finally he hands over a sealed letter: “My grandmother Reine never opened the 1908 letter and never
threw it away. Nor did my father. I'm giving it to you; you open it, it's your job.” The letter is
opened in front of the persons appearing (item 27). Mr Goémard reads it twice.

*Question:* Do you know of a Blaise, in your family or on the island?
*Answer from Mr Goémard:* “I don't know any Blaise.”
*Answer from Mr Cormaud:* “Nor do I. My mother used to say there had been a Goémard son put off the
shore, before her time. His name wasn't spoken.”

*On the fireplace in the Hôtel Garnaud,* Mr Cormaud states that his mother had seen Mrs Marest's drawing,
and that she had said: “A real mark of ours.”

Having heard this read over, they confirm it and sign.
