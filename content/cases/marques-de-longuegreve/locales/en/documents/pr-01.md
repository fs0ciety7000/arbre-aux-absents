::stamp[THE AUBRELIEU COURIER — FRIDAY 3 JANUARY 1862 — 2 SOUS]

## News from the coast — Wreck of the *Aurore*

We hear from Port-Sénane that the schooner *Aurore*, of that port, master Hervé Lannic, returning from
Tennok with a cargo of timber and a few passengers, was lost on Christmas night on the Longue Grève, to
the west of the island of Longuegrève, in a south-west gale of the utmost violence.

The islanders, who ran down by the light of lanterns, could save no one of the crew. Five dead are
counted, whom the sea gave back the following day and whom the island people buried in their cemetery,
unable to tell their names, the ship's papers having been lost.

One single soul survived: a child of about a year old, found alive in a crate the sea had carried up on
to the sand. A carpenter of the island has taken him in. No one at Port-Sénane could say which
passengers the schooner had taken on at Tennok. The child is said to be sturdy, and the island
determined to keep him.

This is the third wreck on the Longue Grève in ten years. Is it not time a light was lit there?
