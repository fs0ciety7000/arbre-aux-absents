| LG-64-06 | A | Saturday 5 September 1964 | Les Cabanes d'Ambre, the Friendly Society's boatshed | Marin Cormaud, aged 44; Rose Pellerin, née Oyard, aged 54 | 22'40 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. Inventory of the marked objects kept in the shed. Plate III of the notebook,
photographs of the same day.*

[00'12] **MARIN.** Everything that was saved in 1908 is here, more or less. People took what they could
carry. We put it in the shed when the Friendly Society was founded.

[00'40] **ROSE.** That one's my father's oar, Elzéar. Elzéar Oyard, the Company's boatman. Look at the
mark: the Eye, and two notches. He couldn't do anything else, but that, he did it clean.

[02'05] **MARIN.** And there, grandfather Noël's oar. Noël Cormaud. With the bar: he carved it in '72,
it's written beside it. My father Lazare's has the bar and a notch underneath, but him, he could sign. He
carved his mark on the oar to do like his father, that's all. Lazare Cormaud, he was a fisherman, he'd
been to school.

[03'40] **ROSE.** And that one's Gatien's. Gatien Goémard, died in '44. Same thing, he signed, but he put
his Fork on his oar, with two strokes.

[05'10] **MARIN.** And that, madam, is old Ursule's netting needle, the net mender. A mending needle, in
boxwood. My grandmother Honorine had it with the nets, when Ursule died: with us, the trade goes from
the old woman to the young one. She added nothing to it. It's Ursule's mark.

[07'20] **ROSE.** The spindle is old Reine's. The bread stamp is Ernestine, the oven. We marked each
house's bread before it went in the oven, that way we knew.

[08'45] **MARIN.** The crate lid is Fulbert, for the seaweed. And the lintel, there, against the wall,
that's from the keeper's house at the light. Benoît and the mistress. The mistress had it taken out the day before the
burning and loaded on the boat. The constables said nothing.

[10'30] **MAREST.** Mrs Pellerin, what do you do?

[10'32] **ROSE.** Me? I've been selling fish at the hall for thirty years. *[laughs]* I haven't got an oar.
Rose Pellerin, at the fish hall, everybody knows me. Rose Oyard, here. Depends which side of the water.
