| LG-64-05 | A and B | Saturday 29 August 1964 | Island of Longuegrève, cemetery | Victoire Cormaud, née Oyard, aged 71; Félix Goémard, aged 52, fishing skipper; S. Marest | 41'20 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. Crossing on Mr Goémard's boat, with the Company's permit. Strong westerly
wind: half the tape is drowned out. I transcribe only what is certain.*

[00'20] **FÉLIX.** *[wind]* … hold her by the arm, madam, the ground's soft in there.

[00'31] **VICTOIRE.** I know the ground, Félix. *[wind]* There. Longuegrève cemetery. Nobody's been put in
it since 1908. The crosses are oak, look: we're lucky, the salt keeps them.

[01'45] **MAREST.** Can you read them for me?

[01'47] **VICTOIRE.** I'll tell them to you. They don't say the name, they say the mark. You carve on the
dead man the mark he used to make: it's his cross, it's him. *[footsteps]* That one, that's Gervais.
Gervais, the *garnaud*. [*Garnaud*: amber gatherer on the shore; by extension, someone with a lucky
hand. — S. M.] The best, people said. Next to him, Perrine, his wife.

[03'10] **VICTOIRE.** There, Clovis. The pilot. There, old Toinette, the midwife, who delivered me… no, it
was my mother who delivered me, Toinette was too old. Toinette held me after.

[04'25] **VICTOIRE.** Siméon. Ursule, up against him. Lubin. Apolline, the oven: she baked the bread for
the whole island, there was one stamp per house.

[06'00] **VICTOIRE.** Those two together, that's Jean and Yves, Siméon's boys. Together, the way they went.
And the little one, there, ever so little, that's little Marthe, my mother's sister. Died at eleven.

[07'40] **VICTOIRE.** Here there's nobody underneath. That's Ange Oyard, the second son of Clovis, lost at sea off
Tennok. They made him a cross all the same. And over there, at the end, the two without a head, with
just the bar: the people of the *Aurore*. Nobody knew their names.

[08'50] **MAREST.** And your father?

[08'53] **VICTOIRE.** My father's there, by Clovis. What's left of his cross. It was deal, and little
Anne's too, beside it: the winter of 1908, there was no more time to make oak, Noël had other things to
do. Deal, the salt eats it. There's nothing left to read. The old ones, Siméon and Noël had made those,
from the oak of the *Aurore*, as it happens.

[09'30] **MAREST.** Mr Goémard, do you come often?

[09'33] **FÉLIX.** *[wind]* Never. We're not allowed. I go past, in the boat, when I go to the pots. My
boat, she's the *Saint-Gervais*. My father said it was for the great-grandfather, that one, Gervais
Goémard. I never knew him, naturally.

[10'50] **VICTOIRE.** He looked like you, Félix. He had your eyebrows. All the Goémards have those
eyebrows, you'd think they were surprised their whole lives long.

*[11'30 – 41'20: tracing of the fourteen legible crosses, cenotaph included (plate II of the notebook);
wind; return to the boat. Mrs Cormaud asks to be left alone a moment in front of Clovis's cross.]*
