| LG-64-07 | A | Monday 17 August 1964 | Port-Sénane, Amber Company, drawing room of the Hôtel Garnaud | Edmée Garnaud, aged 43, president of the Company | 15'15 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. First interview of the survey, obtained for the permit to visit the island.
Miss Garnaud read over the transcript and approved it.*

[00'05] **EDMÉE.** You want the history of the Amber Company? It is short. My grandfather, Blaise
Garnaud, was a child of Port-Sénane. An orphan. He started as a ship's boy, on the packet boats, and he
finished as an amber merchant, with a medal at the Grand Exhibition. He founded the Company in 1886. My
father, Léandre Garnaud, ran it after him. I have run it since 1949.

[01'40] **MAREST.** Did your grandfather know the island people?

[01'43] **EDMÉE.** He bought their amber from the very first day. At the going rate, without haggling: that
was his rule, it is written in the registers. And when the island was evacuated, in 1908, it was he who
housed them at the Cabanes and gave them work. The island people were very well treated. You may ask
them.

[03'20] **MAREST.** The Company's mark, on your labels…

[03'22] **EDMÉE.** The trident. A fork with three prongs, the fishermen's fork of the old days. My
grandfather loved the sea. It is the only fancy he ever allowed himself, along with the motto: “What
amber keeps, it keeps whole.” It was my grandmother who found it, I believe. She was a bookseller.

[05'10] **MAREST.** Would you allow me to trace the fireplace in this room? There is a mark carved…

[05'14] **EDMÉE.** No. *[silence]* Forgive me. No, I would rather not. This is a private house as much as
an office. That fireplace is an architect's whim, there is nothing to be said about it.

*[05'50 – 15'15: the Company's figures (Cabanes workshop, forty workers), conditions of the crossing
permit. End of the interview.]*
