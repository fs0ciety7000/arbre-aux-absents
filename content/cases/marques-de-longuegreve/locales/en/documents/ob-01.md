*Investigator's description, 12 October 1977.*

Ash oar, 3.20 m long, narrow old-style blade, in the manner of the island boats. The grip is worn hollow
by hands, almost black. On the flat of the blade, at hand height, a mark carved with a knife, deep, its
edges softened by use: ::mark[lg-portrait-84365f]. Below it, burned in with a red-hot iron: “1872”.

Mr Marin Cormaud states: “My grandfather Noël's oar. He made it himself, at twelve, the year of his
first amber, with the wood Siméon had given him. He rowed with it to the end. He left it to me; I was
eleven.”

He adds, running his finger along the stroke across it: “That's the bar. That's what they gave the ones
the sea brought. He was proud of it.”
