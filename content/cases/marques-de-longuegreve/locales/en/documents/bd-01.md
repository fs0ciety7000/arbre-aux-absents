| LG-64-01 | A | Saturday 22 August 1964 | Les Cabanes d'Ambre, Mrs Cormaud's kitchen | Victoire Cormaud, née Oyard, aged 71 | 24'10 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. Words of the island speech are in italics; my notes in square brackets.*

[00'05] **MAREST.** Mrs Cormaud, you told me yesterday that you could read the marks.

[00'12] **VICTOIRE.** Read, that's your word. We knew them, that's all. The way you know a face. *[sound
of a cup]*

[00'40] **MAREST.** How did you receive your mark?

[00'44] **VICTOIRE.** *At your first amber.* Round about twelve, the first westerly gale when they let you
go down to the shore with the big ones, in the evening, your father carved the mark for you on the
handle of the basket. And after that you made it yourself, everywhere, on your things. [*First amber*:
a child's first gathering of amber, at about twelve. — S. M.]

[01'30] **MAREST.** And was it the same as the father's?

[01'33] **VICTOIRE.** The same and a bit more. The first boy, a stroke on the left. The second, a stroke on
the right. And the third… the third had no strip, he went off without a mark. He went to the
Grand-Terre, or on the boats. *[silence]* There weren't many of them, third ones.

[02'50] **MAREST.** Your house, that was…

[02'52] **VICTOIRE.** The Eye. Us Oyards, we're the Eye. My grandfather, Clovis Oyard, he was a pilot, he
had the Eye all on its own, the full mark, because he was head of the house when the surveyor came. My
father, **Médard Oyard**, the boatman, had the Eye and a notch on the left: he was Clovis's eldest. He's
the one who ran the island boat, people, amber, the post.

[04'15] **MAREST.** And your brothers?

[04'18] **VICTOIRE.** I had one brother, Elzéar. **Elzéar Oyard.** Two notches on the left, one under the
other: the eldest of the eldest. And he never would sign. The mistress had him three days at the
Longuegrève school, the first year, and then my father took him on the boat. He used to say school was
all right for girls and for the light. *[laughs]* He made his mark on his pay, at the Company, right to
the end.

[06'02] **MAREST.** And your mother?

[06'05] **VICTOIRE.** My mother, Jacquette Goémard. She had my father's mark, the Eye and the notch, and
underneath, at the foot, a little Fork, because she was born a Goémard. We called that the *heel*. A
married woman, you could tell by her heel. [*Heel*: small head of the house of birth, under the foot of
a wife's mark. — S. M.]

[07'30] **MAREST.** And before she was married?

[07'33] **VICTOIRE.** Before, a girl had her father's mark with a grain. A little hole, there. One for the
first girl, two for the second. My mother, before my father, she'd had the Fork and one grain: she was
the elder of Gervais's girls. I was told that, I never saw it.

[09'10] **MAREST.** And you?

[09'12] **VICTOIRE.** Me, I sign. It was the mistress taught me. I never had a mark. I know them all and I
haven't got one. *[laughs]* That's how we ended up, us lot: knowing how to read what we can't make any
more.

[10'40 – 24'10] *[Mrs Cormaud describes the four heads, the mark of the heads of house and that of the
sea children; see plate I, where I have set down her explanations. End of side A.]*
