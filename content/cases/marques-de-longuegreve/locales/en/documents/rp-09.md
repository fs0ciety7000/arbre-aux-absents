::stamp[NATURE RESERVES SERVICE — NOTE No. 76-14]

**Subject:** ornithological interest of the island of Longuegrève (Golfe d'Ambre); proposal for
designation as a nature reserve.

**1. Birdlife.** The island, uninhabited since 1908 and visited only by the licensed amber gatherers,
shelters the largest colony of **Sandwich terns** in the gulf (about 900 pairs in June 1976), a small
colony of **puffins** in the east cliff (35 burrows occupied) and breeding **oystercatchers** all
along the Longue Grève. The disturbance caused by gathering amber after storms is the only threat
observed.

**2. Present state.** The ruins of the hamlet occupy the centre of the island: fourteen dry-stone
enclosures, without roofs. **Four fireplace lintels still in place bear a carved mark: a fork, a
sort of diamond like an eye, a chevron like an arrow, a gallows.** The cemetery, enclosed by its wall,
is looked after now and then by people from the Cabanes; wooden crosses. The gas light on the pointe
du Feu is working.

**3. Proposal.** Designation of the whole island as a nature reserve, prohibition of amber gathering
from March to August. The land status needs clarifying with the State Lands: according to the
cadastre, the island still belongs to four families evacuated in 1908.
