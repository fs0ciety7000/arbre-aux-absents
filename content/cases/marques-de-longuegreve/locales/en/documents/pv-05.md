**RECORD OF HEARING**

On Wednesday 19 October 1977, at ten o'clock in the morning, at the Office, Aubrelieu, before us,
permanent rapporteur, assisted by the clerk, there appeared on summons:

**Mrs Solange Marest**, aged fifty-three, senior lecturer at the Laboratory of Speech and Customs of the
University of Aubrelieu.

**On the 1964 survey.** “I went to the Cabanes for the island words, not for the marks. It was Mrs
Cormaud who taught me to read them, in three weeks. She used to say: ‘It isn't writing, it's figures.’
I traced everything, then I wrote her grammar down in my notebook.”

**On the notation.** “I noted with the dot and the slash, with a plus for the heel. Your machines want
letters; your department has put them in. I have nothing against it, so long as people don't read the
letters instead of the marks.”

**On the exception of the bar.** “Victoire insisted on that more than on the rest. The sea child bears
the bar: *not of the blood, but of the house*. She repeated the sentence three times, so that I would
write it down right.”

**On the fireplace in the Hôtel Garnaud.** “I saw it for one minute, from the drawing-room door. Miss
Garnaud had me shown out. I drew it from memory. When I showed it to Victoire, she said it was a real
mark, and she would not speak of it again. I did not know how to read the rest, at the time. Today I
do.”

**On the expert report.** Mrs Marest agrees to examine for the Office the Fork-shaped marks with a
right-hand notch in the file, and to compare the originals.

Having heard this read over, she confirms it and signs.

::signature[S. Marest — the rapporteur — the clerk]
