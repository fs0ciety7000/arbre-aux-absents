| LG-64-03 | A | Monday 24 August 1964 | Les Cabanes d'Ambre, the Friendly Society's shed | Victoire Cormaud, née Oyard, aged 71 | 19'30 |

*Transcript by S. Marest. Mrs Cormaud talks about her father-in-law.*

[00'08] **MAREST.** Tell me about your father-in-law.

[00'11] **VICTOIRE.** Noël Cormaud. My husband's father. He died in '31, he was seventy or thereabouts,
nobody knew for sure. He was a sea child. [*Sea child*: a child found alive on the shore and brought up
by a house of the island. — S. M.]

[01'02] **MAREST.** Go on.

[01'04] **VICTOIRE.** On Christmas night 1861, a schooner from Port-Sénane, the *Aurore*, came and lay
down on the Longue Grève. A gale, at night. The men went down with the lanterns. There were five dead,
they buried them with the bar on the cross, since nobody knew their names. And in a crate, on the
shore, there was something crying. The crying crate, people said. That was him. One year old, about.

[03'15] **MAREST.** Who took him in?

[03'17] **VICTOIRE.** Siméon. Siméon Cormaud, head of the Arrow. He was a shipwright, he built the boats
for the whole island. He took him in, old Toinette opened the crate, and that was that. They called
him Noël, because of the night. And when he came of age, they barred his foot. That was the rule: the
Arrow, since he was of the house, and the bar, since he wasn't of the blood. He never complained of it.
He built boats like Siméon, better, the old ones said.

[06'40] **MAREST.** Did Siméon have sons?

[06'42] **VICTOIRE.** Two. Jean and Yves. Siméon's two real boys drowned together in the westerly gale of
'69, gathering amber too late. After that, Ursule, his wife, never spoke again. She kept Noël as her
son. And Noël took a wife from the Lestauds: Honorine, Honorine Lestaud, she was Lubin's younger girl.

[09'05] **MAREST.** And the Cormaud house, after Siméon?

[09'08] **VICTOIRE.** *[silence]* Now that's a story. When Siméon was ill, the winter of '90, he sent for
the Father, the priest, and four marks, the four heads, like for a wedding. And he said in front of
them that Noël was his son before God and that he would have the strip. The Father wrote it all down.
We called that a shore testament. The Father took it to the Port-Sénane notary, and then nobody ever
heard of it again. Noël never went. He used to say: “I didn't know you had to.”

[12'30] **MAREST.** Nobody disputed the house with him?

[12'33] **VICTOIRE.** Dispute what? We were four houses on an island. He built the boats, he led the Arrow
until the evacuation, and afterwards at the Cabanes. It's the Grand-Terre that didn't know, not us.
The Cormaud strip, in the cadastre, it stayed in Siméon's name. My son says it's still there.
