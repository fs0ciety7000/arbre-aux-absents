Longuegrève, Sunday 15 March 1908

My dear Berthe,

There is no boat before the 1st; I shall give my letter to the Company's launch if it comes back.

The district doctor came yesterday, on Mr Garnaud's launch. He stayed five hours. He saw Clément's two
little ones, who had the measles in February and now run about like rabbits, and old Jacquette took him
into every house. He looked at throats, he tasted the well water, he asked how many deaths since the
winter. They told him: little Anne, of the croup, at Christmas, and Médard, who had been coughing for
years. He saw nothing, Berthe, because there was nothing.

And yet there is talk of a certificate. The constable came by this morning to tell old Josse that they
would have to leave. Nobody understands. Benoît says it is the shore they want.

I am copying out the chapel register, at night, into the school exercise books, so that they do not
burn our names too. The priest agrees; he leaves it with me in the evening and takes it back in the
morning. Benoît holds the lamp and tells me the names that can no longer be read. I have got to 1876.

Say nothing to Mama.

Your sister,

::signature[Sidonie]
