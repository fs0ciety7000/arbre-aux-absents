::stamp[CABANES FRIENDLY SOCIETY — LES CABANES D'AMBRE, BY PORT-SÉNANE]

Les Cabanes d'Ambre, Monday 26 September 1977

**To the Director of the Civil Re-registration Office**

Dear Sir,

We have read the State Lands notice in *The Echo*. We are the grandchildren and the great-grandchildren
of the people of Longuegrève. We write to you on behalf of the forty-seven persons whose list is
enclosed, and on behalf of those who died at the Cabanes without seeing the island again.

**Our grandparents were driven out by a fever that nobody had.** Our parents told us so, and they had
it from those who were on the jetty on 2 April 1908. We will be told it is a family story. We ask that
it be checked.

We do not ask for the State Lands' money. We do not want to stand in the way of the birds: Miss
Lestaud, who counts them, is one of us; we learned it this month, and so did she. We ask that the
island remain with the four houses, and for the right to be buried in **Longuegrève cemetery**, where
our dead lie until 1908 and where no one has been buried since.

My mother, Mrs Victoire Cormaud, used to say that you do not sell the shore. She died in 1970. She
spoke to the University in 1964; it is all there, recorded.

For the Friendly Society:

::signature[Marin Cormaud, president, foreman at the Cabanes workshop]

::signature[Félix Goémard, fishing skipper, vice-president]

::signature[Rose Pellerin, née Rose Oyard, treasurer]
