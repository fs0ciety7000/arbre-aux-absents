## [commun] The Commission

On Monday 14 November 1977, the joint Office–State Lands Commission sat in Aubrelieu on file
No. 77-0874. The table held: thirty-two slots, each on two documents, and nineteen of them bore a mark
instead of a face. The chairman, who had never seen one, asked to have the first read to him. They
read him Gervais Goémard's Fork, as he had made it for the surveyor in 1868; then that of his son
Josse, with the notch on the left; then the other, with the notch on the right. The chairman said:
“I see.” He looked for a long time at the younger son's slot.

The report hid nothing of the Garnaud branch. Blaise Garnaud, founder of the Amber Company, “a child
of Port-Sénane” according to his obituary, had been born a Goémard on Longuegrève on 3 February 1854,
Gervais's younger son; the same hand had drawn the same mark on the roll of ship's boys in 1871 and
at the foot of his testament in 1921. Consequently, Miss Edmée Garnaud, president of the Company and
objector in the file, was Gervais Goémard's great-granddaughter and appeared on the list of
beneficiaries of the house of Goémard, on the same footing as Mr Félix Goémard. Maître Joubaud rose to
object; his client touched his arm, and he sat down again.

Mr Marin Cormaud, for the Cabanes Friendly Society, said only one sentence: “We never asked for the
money.” Mrs Rose Pellerin, beside him, added that she was sixty-seven and would like to know before
the winter where she would be buried. Miss Aline Lestaud said nothing; she had brought her note of
1976, and did not take it out of her bag.

## [s1] The paper fever

To the question of the evacuation, the report answered without hedging: there had been no fever on
Longuegrève. The certificate of Friday 13 March 1908 claimed a visit “this day”; the launch *Trident*
had stayed alongside that day, weatherbound by a gale, and had crossed only on the Saturday. The
chairman read for himself, in Dr Pélissart's rounds register, the line for 14 March: “No fever.
Nothing to report.” He read it twice, the second time in a lower voice.

The report also bore a name that was in no petition: that of Sidonie Lestaud, née Brémoy,
schoolmistress, who had copied the chapel register by night before it was burned, and who had written
at the foot of her last notebook: “None of these dead died of the fever.” Mrs Sollier, sitting in the
back row, had put on her Sunday coat.

The concession of the shores runs until 31 May 1983. The Commission recommended that it not be
renewed; the State Lands took note.

## [s3] The shore testament

The house of Cormaud had had no descendant by blood since the westerly gale of 1869. The report
produced the original deed of 16 February 1890: before Father Rabier and four marks, Siméon Cormaud
had declared Noël, the child of the *Aurore*, “his son before God” and the heir to his strip. The
Commission took note of it. The Cormaud strip was entered in the name of the descendants of Noël
Cormaud, and Maître Gilbert Lhermet, grandson of the notary of 1890, at last registered the deed his
grandfather had filed away. He said it was the oldest outstanding matter in the practice, and that he
was glad to close it.

In the spring, Marin Cormaud took his grandfather's oar over to the island and leaned it against
Siméon's cross.

## [s3-non] The Cormaud share

For want of an established beneficiary for the house of Cormaud, the share of the Cormaud strip
passes to the State, which becomes a joint owner.

## [s4] The banished son

The report said in one sentence why Blaise Goémard had left the island: in 1870, he had said that he
had taken the amber from the Lestaud strip so that his elder brother, Josse, who had taken it, could
keep the house; he had been put off the shore in his place, and had never told anyone.

Félix Goémard read the 1908 letter aloud, later, in the island cemetery, in front of Miss Garnaud,
who had asked to come. He stopped at “Your brother” and could not finish; she finished for him. Then,
on a new cross beside Gervais's, they carved for Blaise the Fork with the notch on the right. Félix
said, putting away his knife: “He had kept it.”

## [conclusion] The compensation

The Commission settled the list of beneficiaries of the four heads of the 1868 roll. The island of
Longuegrève passed to the State against compensation, for the reserve. The two hundred and
ninety-five thousand new florins were divided by house, then by head, according to the table. The
State Lands had a notice put up at the slipway: “Longuegrève Nature Reserve — no access from 1 March
to 31 August”.

Safe-deposit box No. 108 did not enter the report. The Savings Bank closed it again; its rent, paid in
advance in 1908, runs until 2008. Before handing the file back, the rapporteur slipped into it an index
card in the rapporteur's own hand: “Box 108 — to be looked at again.”

At the Cabanes, Félix Goémard repainted the name of the *Saint-Gervais*. He said to the investigator
who had come to return his photographs: “The money, we'll take. The island, we'll look at it from the
jetty.”
